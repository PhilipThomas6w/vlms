## Varangians

The Varangians were highly trained, loyal soldiers who protected the Byzantine Emperors for over 400 years, from about 988AD to about 1400AD. Many of them were Vikings.

In 1066 William the Conqueror invaded England. He destroyed towns and villages. He also introduced a different version of Christianity. Many English families lost their homes and were not happy with the western ways of worshipping God so they moved to Constantinople. Some of the men joined the elite Varangian Guard.

The Varangian Guard’s most important duty was to protect the Emperor wherever he went. They were the Emperor’s body guard. When he left the Palace a group of the Guard would go with him whether it was to the Great Church of Hagia Sophia or to the hippodrome for the races or for processions through the streets.

The Guardsmen came from Scandinavia and from Slavic states as well as from England.

Our club is named after this elite Military Unit as, although there is no longer a Byzantine Emperor, we try to be faithful and loyal to our Heavenly King, Almighty God, and protect our Christian Faith from enemy attack.

## St Theodore the Recruit

### St Theodore Tyron, Great Martyr

Tyro means ‘Recruit’. We have recruits in our Varangian Guard Club. It means they have joined up.

No sooner had St Theodore joined the army than a fierce persecution of Christians started under the pagan Emperor Maximian. Theodore did not hide the fact that he was a Christian and believed in Jesus Christ. He encouraged the Christian soldiers to witness to their belief and refused to worship pagan idols when his commander sent the order. Instead he went to the pagan temple during the night, set fire to the idol and reduced it to ashes proving that it had no power. As a result, he was brought to trial and put in prison. Our Lord Jesus Christ appeared to St Theodore in prison and told him he did not need to take any more food or drink as he would be with Him in heaven very soon. He was then tortured and burnt on a stake. Although he died in the fire his body was not burnt. This was on the 17th February 306. Many Churches were built in his memory.

About 60 years later the pagan Emperor Julian the Apostate, who was desperately trying to bring back Pagan customs, tried to stop the Christians keeping the Great Fast. He ordered that all the fasting food in the market place should be replaced by food that had been sprinkled with blood from animals sacrificed to pagan idols. All the Christians gathered with their Patriarch Eudoxios to pray all night. God heard their prayer and St Theodore appeared to Patriarch Eudoxios. When the Archbishop asked him who he was he replied: I am the martyr, listen to me. Boil wheat and distribute it among the people and you will be saved from the polluted food. Ever since then Orthodox Christians have brought boiled wheat to Church on the first Saturday in Lent and made Kolyva.

### Troparion (Tone 2)

Great are the achievements of faith!

Holy Martyr Theodore rejoiced in the fountain of the flames as though in still waters of rest; consumed in the fire, he was offered as a sacrifice to the Trinity. At his intercessions Christ our God, do Thou save our souls.

### Kolyva

**Ingredients:** wheat, chopped nuts, dried fruit. You can also add pomegranate, parsley, honey or icing sugar.

**Method**

- Boil the wheat in water until it is soft (this will take an hour or two).
- Drain and then dry the wheat in a tea towel.
- Mix in the other ingredients.
- Place in a bowl and decorate.
- Bring your kolyva to Church with a list of the names of those who have died that you are remembering.

**What the ingredients mean**

- **Wheat** has to die and be buried before it rises into a new plant.
- **Nuts** show that life is hard.
- **Fruit** is for the fruit of this life.
- **Honey or sugar** is for the sweetness of Eternal Life.

### Kolyva, Romanian tradition

**Ingredients:** polished wheat called Arpacas, sugar, fine bread crumbs, lemon zest with sugar, cocoa, grated walnuts, dried grated coconut, chopped nuts, rum, dried fruit and sweets.

**Method**

- Cover the Arpacas with 10cm of water above the wheat.
- Boil until it is soft and the water has been absorbed.
- Allow to cool for a couple of hours.
- Mix in a bowl with the grated walnuts, fine bread crumbs, rum, lemon zest with sugar, and more sugar to taste.
- Decorate with walnuts, coconut, dried fruit, and sweets.
- Make a cross in the centre with cocoa using sugar for the rays.
- Bring your kolyva to Church with a list of the names of those who have died that you are remembering.
