## The Crusades

- **1071** Battle of Manzikert: the Roman army was routed by the Seljuk Turks.
- By **1081**, when Alexius I came to the throne, the whole of Asia Minor was lost.

Alexius appealed to the Pope for mercenaries to drive back the Turks. It is generally written that instead, he got the First Crusade. We now know that this is incorrect. The First Crusade, 1098, started as a large mercenary army intended to free Asia Minor from the Turks, hence that erratic route across Asia Minor. In the meantime, a large Byzantine army and navy freed ports along the western seaboard of Asia Minor and cleared the Turks out of the Aegean Sea. Things went wrong however, after the siege of Antioch, when the Crusaders ignored their commitment to Alexios and marched south to take Jerusalem.

By **1099** Antioch, Jerusalem and the entire Levant coast was taken by the Crusaders.

But to maintain their position they needed reinforcements. Hence:

- Second Crusade (1147–1149)
- Third Crusade (1191–1192) and
- Fourth Crusade (1202–1204)

This latter turned aside on their way to the Levant and attacked Constantinople, took it, and replaced the Byzantine Emperor with a Latin Emperor.

From this time the Church in Rome has been separated from the Orthodox Church.

## The First Crusade

The First Crusade was not an easy journey. The armies ran out of food and fresh water before they even reached the Levant, but the locals would only sell them food and water for extremely high prices. Many soldiers had to drink urine, animal blood or sewage water, and drinking unclean water often gave the crusaders lethal and contagious diseases. Diseases like dysentery, the severe infection of the intestines that causes serious diarrhoea, were considered normal. Eventually the crusaders began to pillage and steal food from any poorly defended countryside town they reached; they believed that this was right because they were doing a service for God and they needed food and water to keep them going. This gave the crusaders a very bad reputation, and the Emperor of the Byzantine Empire was very unhappy because of it.
