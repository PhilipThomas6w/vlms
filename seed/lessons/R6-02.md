## The Epitaphios

The Epitaphios is a rectangular cloth on which there is a depiction of the body of Jesus laid out for burial. There are also figures of Mary, the Mother of God and Joseph of Arimathea on the cloth.

![Icon of the Lamentation at the Tomb, with the words “Lamentation at the tomb”: the Mother of God cradles the head of the dead Christ, who lies on a cloth-covered slab; Joseph of Arimathea, Nicodemus, Saint John and the myrrh-bearing women mourn beneath the dark cross, with a ladder against it](epitaphios-icon.jpg)

During the service of Vespers on Great and Holy Friday Christ’s body is taken from the cross, which will be standing in the centre of the church. It is wrapped in a white cloth and placed on the altar. This is the sign of his burial by Joseph of Arimathea.

Later the Priest lifts the Epitaphios off the altar and carries it in a procession around the church. He places it amongst the flowers of a beautifully decorated funeral bier. The people gather round this bier and sing the hymns of Lamentation, expressing their sorrow over Jesus’ death in the words of a Mother to her dead Son.

Everyone who is at the service follow the Epitaphios out of the church on a funeral procession, carrying a lighted candle and singing ‘Holy God…’

Before they leave the Church they kiss the body of Jesus in the tomb and are given a flower to take home to their icon corner.

## The burial of Christ

> At the setting of the sun, Joseph of Arimathea, a secret disciple of the Lord, goes to Pilate and begs from him the body of the Saviour. Having been given permission, Joseph, with another secret disciple, Nicodemos, go to the Cross of the Lord. They lovingly take down His precious and life-giving Body. With lamentation, they anoint and wrap Him, and place Him in Joseph’s own newly carved tomb, rolling a great stone over its entrance.

### Holy Friday

Holy Friday is a day of mourning, fasting and prayer. We commemorate the Passion of Jesus Christ. On this day, our Lord went to the Cross and died to take away our sins. The power of death and the reality of evil rule the world on this dreadful day. Yet Jesus Christ’s death marks the beginning of His, and our, victory over death’s power.

This solemn service is Matins of Friday, sung on Thursday evening. It consists of:

- **The Twelve Gospel Readings.** These narratives from the four Gospels relate the events of Jesus’ Holy Passion, and His last instructions to the disciples.
- **The Procession.** In some churches, after the fifth Gospel, the crucifix is adorned with a flower wreath and carried in procession. This symbolises Christ’s coming to Golgotha to offer Himself as sacrifice for the world’s sins.

![Part of a line drawing: a priest carrying a crucifix wreathed in flowers and candles in procession, with servers carrying candles and a censer, and people in the pews](holy-friday-procession.jpg)

### Friday morning and afternoon

The Friday morning services, or “Royal Hours”, retell the story of Christ’s Passion with the addition of prophecies, psalms and hymns. They help us keep vigil at the side of the crucified Christ, and relate Jesus’ suffering to our own redemption.

The Friday afternoon service is a vespers (the Apokathilosis) marking the beginning of Holy Saturday:

- In many churches, the priest takes Christ’s body from the Cross, wraps it in a white cloth and places it on the altar, as a sign of His burial by Joseph of Arimathea.
- A cloth icon or shroud depicting Christ’s sacred body, called the Epitaphios or Plaschanittsa, is carried in procession and placed in a flowered bier that represents Christ’s tomb.

The Gospel readings during the Friday afternoon service are Matthew 27:1-38, Luke 23:39-43, Matthew 27:39-54, John 19:31-37 and Matthew 27:55-61.

![Line drawing: a priest and two servers carry the Epitaphios, a cloth held up between them, towards a bier decorated with flowers and topped with a cross and four candles](epitaphios-and-bier.jpg)

### Holy Saturday

On Holy Friday evening, the Matins of Holy Saturday are sung. At this service we sing the Hymns of the Lamentations. We lament Jesus’ undeserved death for our salvation. With both sorrow and joy we sing the Lamentations (Praises) to Him who is symbolically buried, yet who we already know is the risen Lord and Giver of Life. At this service, the Epitaphios is taken in a candlelight procession around the church.

> “In a tomb they laid Thee, O Christ the Life. By Thy Death Thou hast cast down the might of death and become the font of life for all the world.”

The Gospel reading during the Lamentation service is Matthew 27:62-66.
