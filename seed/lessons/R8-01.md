## Matins (Orthros, which literally means Daybreak)

On Sundays this service proclaims our redemption from the fallen state that finds us trapped with Adam, in the clutches of Satan and remaining in hades for eternity. Now the gates of hades have been trampled down and Heaven is opened to us. We are once again able to live eternally in the presence of God as Adam did in Paradise.

While still in darkness we hear the six psalms recited at the beginning of Matins expressing our faith in God our Saviour, recognising our sinfulness, confessing and asking, forgiveness, and the deliverance from our enemies round about us.

Dawn is the time for the service of Matins, (Orthros in Greek). As the sun rises, we think of God enlightening the world. “Glory to God….” Sing the angels on hearing about our Saviour’s birth in the night. Here is the Light, not only of the dawn but a greater inner Light which enlightens our faith. There is a sense of joy as the service tells of the Resurrection, a Feast or of a Saint for which an appropriate Gospel is appointed.

By using ancient hymns from the Old Testament and those composed by Christian hymnographers the service of Matins intertwines God’s saving acts such as the Israelites escape from the Egyptians through crossing the Red Sea on dryland, the release from captivity in Babylon.

The Prophets experiences of God in their lives foretold how redemption would become a reality. Isaiah, Habakkuk, Jonah, Daniel and the three Holy Children in the furnace and other passages from scripture prefigure Christ’s Resurrection from the dead, discovered early in the morning by the faithful Myrrhbearing women.

Before the Great Doxology is sung at the conclusion of Matins, there are the Praises: “Let everything that hath breath praise the Lord….”

“The Magnificat,” the hymn sung by the Mother of God when she visited her cousin Elizabeth, sums up the essence of Matins.

The structure of this service is used for other special services throughout the year.

## The Prophets: Habakkuk, Isaiah and Jonah Show the Resurrection of Christ

*Typed from a handout page.*

From the Holy Saturday Canon (Good Friday)

### The Prayer of Habakkuk, Chapter 3:1-19

‘Foreseeing your divine self-emptying on the Cross, Avvakoum [Habakkuk], amazed, cried out, “You cut off the might of the powerful, O Good One, you speaking with those in Hell as all-powerful”.’

> His brightness will be like the light;  
> Horns will be in His hand,  
> And He established a mighty love of His strength. (Habakkuk 3:4) SAAS.

![Icon of the Prophet Habakkuk with a halo, turned to listen with his hand raised, holding a scroll that reads “The righteous shall live by faith”, with his name beside him](icon-of-habakkuk.jpg)

### The Prayer of Isaiah, Chapter 26:9-20

‘Isaias, as he watched by night, O Christ, saw the light which knows no evening of your theophany, which in your compassion came to pass for us, and he cried, “The dead will arise and those in graves will rise, and those in earth will rejoice.”’

Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in tombs, bestowing life.

![Icon of the Prophet Isaiah, an elderly bearded man in a green robe with a halo, holding a scroll that reads “He was pierced for our offenses”, with his name at the top](icon-of-isaiah.jpg)

### The Prayer of Jonah, Chapter 2:2-9

‘Jonas was held, but not held fast in the belly of the whale; for being a type of you, the One who suffered and was given over to burial, as from a bridal chamber he leapt forth from the beast and cried to the guard, and you who vainly and falsely keep guard, you have forsaken your own mercy.’

Jonah shows the way to Jesus:

- God said ‘Go to Nineveh’; Jesus said ‘Go therefore and make disciples of all nations.’
- Jonah was in the belly of the whale for three days; Jesus was in the tomb for three days.

In Jonah’s weakness we can see our own weak selves but as he turns to God we can also see a picture to come of Christ where we will be called by name but first we must become servants.
