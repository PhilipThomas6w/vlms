## The Anointing Service

**1. What is a Mystery?**

A Mystery is something we cannot completely understand or explain, like the presence of Christ in Communion.

**2. When is the Anointing Service done in Great Lent in the Greek tradition?**

It is done on Wednesday evening of Great Week (Holy Week). However, people who are ill can be anointed at any time, so the Anointing Service is not only used in Great Week.

**3. Why is the Anointing Service done?**

It is done for physical and spiritual healing.

**4. What does ‘physical and spiritual healing’ mean?**

This means healing of both the body and the soul. You can’t have one without the other. In the end the spiritual healing and forgiveness of sins are more important. It is up to God whether the sick are completely physically cured, but in the end it is how we suffer and die that demonstrates our love of God and ultimate union with Him in the Kingdom of Heaven.

**5. What do we need to do this service?**

During this service we need:

- olive oil in a lamp,
- 7 wands with cotton wool (or sometimes candles are used),
- a bowl of flour,

all placed on a table in the middle of the Church.

![A drawing of the bowl of flour with the oil lamp standing in the middle and seven wands tipped with cotton wool standing in the flour](anointing-bowl-lamp-wands.jpg)

**6. What happens in this service?**

During this service we sing hymns about the saints who have healed. The Priests (ideally 7) read 7 Gospels and 7 prayers to ask God to bless the oil and to grant healing from physical and spiritual sickness. At the end of the service all the Orthodox people are anointed with the oil on their forehead, the palms of their hands and the backs of their hands. (The flour is later used to make a prosphora.)

Note: Bishops never do this service.

![A priest in green vestments anoints the forehead of a worshipper, who holds a lit candle, while others wait in line with candles](anointing-the-faithful.jpg)

**7. Why do we do this service in Great Week?**

We should have kept the fast, been to the Lenten services and been to confession. Now we are asking God to make us ready to meet the Resurrected Christ at Pascha.
