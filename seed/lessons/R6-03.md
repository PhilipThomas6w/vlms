## What Jesus said

The Gospel of St John 20:22-23:

> *And when He had said this, He breathed on them, and said to them, “Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven; if you retain the sins of any they are retained.”*

## Making a confession

Ask for God’s help in making a confession.

1. Find a quiet time to ask God to show you what sort of a person you are and ask what He would really like you to be. How could you change your ways?
2. You should be really sorry and ashamed and try very hard not to repeat the same thing.
3. It is a good thing to confess your sins in church to show God you are really sorry. Most people do this before a big feast.
4. You are admitting your sins to God. The priest is only there as a witness to represent all the Christians and give you absolution, so that you know that God has forgiven you. (It is helpful to write a list so that you don’t forget when you are standing in front of the icon with the priest.)
5. You should be confessing your sins, not accidents or other people’s.

## The Mystery of Repentance

*Typed from a handout page (source not identified).*

In the Holy Mystery of Repentance or Confession, God has provided for a concrete means for us to return to Him, to be renewed in His image, and to grow in His grace.

**Confession is not…**

- a way of earning the right to “take communion”
- a counselling session to “work on” our problems
- a private telling God we are sorry so He will not be so angry at us
- a means of “wiping the slate clean” when we have no intention of changing our lives

**Confession is…**

- a bringing of what is happening in our lives to the Light of God’s cleansing grace
- a correction of self-deception and self-righteousness
- a liberation from the pretence of pride
- an opening of our whole being to the healing grace of God
- a part of our preparation for the reception of the Holy Eucharist

Above all, it is the chief means of **reconciliation** with God and **restoration** to His Church.

Confession *renews us* in the image of God, in the purity of our baptism, and in the process of our deification, our communion in Christ with the Holy Trinity.

As we take the opportunity that the Holy Mystery of Confession offers us, we grow in our self-understanding and in the virtues of humility and mercy.

**In Confession, we stand before the icon and the priest stands alongside us.**

- We make the sign of the cross and venerate the icon.
- The priest invites us to confess our sins openly to Christ as a Saviour of kindness, not as a Judge of vengeance.
- We confess our own sins, not anyone else’s.
- We confess openly and genuinely, knowing that nothing is hidden from the Lord, who knows our need for mercy better than we ourselves.
- We try to answer the priest’s questions without excusing or justifying ourselves.
- The priest may offer some spiritual guidance to guide us to grow in the Grace of God.
- To conclude, the priest places the epitrachilion over our head and prays that “the Lord Jesus Christ through His grace and love for mankind will forgive us our sins.”
- We depart firmly trusting that through this Holy Mystery, God has forgiven our sins and we are released, restored, and cleansed.
