## Making the Sign of the Cross

In the Name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.

We make the sign of the Cross often as Orthodox Christians. We use it when we come into church, when we are venerating something, when we want to emphasise a particular prayer. We make the sign of the Cross if we are asking God to bless us, someone or something.

## Using Your Body to Pray

We should use our bodies to pray in other ways too – bowing our heads, touching the ground, kissing something that is holy. We use our ears to hear the words, bells and singing; our noses to smell the incense; eyes to see all the beautiful icons etc.; our mouths to taste the bread and wine and to kiss the icons.

## Lighting Votive Candles

Lighting a candle is an important thing to do for Orthodox Christians.

It reminds us that God is present with us. He came into the world as Jesus Christ. He came to tell us about the Kingdom of Heaven and so we think about Christ as the Light of the World. You can see better with light. You can’t see clearly if it’s dark. Candles and lamps have been used in services since God told Moses to lead the Israelites out of Egypt.

We also light a candle as a prayer. If we want to remember someone especially, we can light a candle for them.

## Sitting and Standing in Services

Man has been created by God to stand on two legs, therefore we usually worship God standing up. However we sometimes bow, do metanias or prostrations. There are even special times when we do kneel to remind ourselves that we are far from perfect.

If you can’t possibly stand up for the whole service because you are very old or ill or holding a baby there are some moments when you could sit down. These are:

- during the reading of the Epistle (Apostolos)
- or when the deacon or Priest are praying the Litanies – “Let us pray to the Lord” and the choir and congregation reply “Lord have mercy” or “Grant this O Lord”

There are some moments in the Service when we should definitely stand, and pay special attention and bow. These are:

- when the Priest gives a blessing
- when the Priest or deacon are censing
- during processions such as when the Gospel Book is brought in (the Little Entrance)

You should never be sitting while:

- the Gospel (Evangelion) is being read
- the Creed (“I believe …”) is being said
- the Priest is saying the prayer asking the Holy Spirit to change the bread and wine into the Body and Blood of Christ (the Consecration)
- we are saying the “Our Father …”
- members of the congregation are taking Communion (even if you are not yourself)

## Receiving Communion

Before you receive Communion, you should venerate *one* icon to show that you are an Orthodox Christian. Then you should stand with your hands across your chest ready and waiting. This shows other people that you intend to receive Communion. It also makes sure that arms don’t knock the chalice (cup) or spoon.

If you have to wait a long time, look at the icons on the screen and the candles and think about God coming to you.

(We do not kiss an icon after the Communion has been brought out of the altar as God is in the Communion so you don’t need to venerate an icon at this time.)

Before you receive Communion, make the sign of the cross. When it is your turn, hold the red cloth under your chin with two hands so that no Communion ever drops on to the floor. Wipe your mouth and then take a small piece of bread to eat to make sure you swallow all the Communion.

Stand quietly and still while everyone else receives.

***You have God in you!***

## Preparation for Receiving Communion

Before coming to church to receive God in the Holy Communion we should prepare ourselves.

Here are some ways:

- make a special effort to be clean and tidy
- gradually learn to have nothing to eat (start by just having a drink and a piece of toast)
- say some of the special Communion prayers
- fast on Wednesdays and Fridays
- when old enough, make regular confessions
