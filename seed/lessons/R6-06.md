## The Prophet Isaiah

Isaiah was probably the most important of all the prophets. Born about 700 years before Christ, he was a national figure in Israel, trying to advise the King. However war broke out which devastated Palestine although Jerusalem was saved at this time.

He was called by God in a vision of God which influenced his understanding of religious ideas (Isaiah 6:1-8). He was a poet of genius and a religious genius. He wrote amazing poetry telling how great God is and how man is unworthy. He said God requires justice between men, and sincere divine worship. God looks for faithfulness- trust in God and no one else- only by this will you gain salvation. He tells of a Messiah, a king, the descendent of David, who will establish peace and justice on earth and through him God will be known.

He prophesied that Christ will come (Isaiah 7:14: a virgin will conceive …).

He prophesied about the Crucifixion (Isaiah 50:5-8).

> THE BOOK OF ISAIAH
>
> **Author:** According to both Jewish and Christian tradition, Isaiah the son of Amoz is the author of this book. Isaiah, which means "the salvation of God”, was from the tribe of Judah and was the nephew of King Amaziah (796-767 BC) of Judah. He is commemorated on May 9.
>
> **Date:** The time period is mentioned as "in the days of Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah, kings of Judah." This places the book about 745-680 BC, from approximately 25 years before the Assyrian captivity of the Northern Kingdom to about 40 years after it.
>
> **Major theme:**  "The Holy One of Israel" (1:4), who punishes His unrepentant people but later will redeem them. The prophet's vision of the glory of God (1:6ff) is one of infinite transcendence. Man's role is one of faith in response to God's holiness and vision for the world. Isaiah revealed the Messiah in amazing detail as if he were telling history instead of the future. He even describes the Theotokos (ch. 7) and how she is to conceive and bear God the Son incarnate with her virginity intact. Isaiah describes the suffering of Christ (ch. 53) together with His Resurrection from the dead, proclaiming His kingdom as if it had already happened.
>
> **Background:** Assyria was growing in power under Tiglath-Pileser, who turned to the west after his conquests to the east. He saw the kingdom of Israel fall to Assyria's might along with other nations along the Mediterranean coast. Seeing his people taken captive, Isaiah turned to warn the Southern Kingdom of Judah to trust in God alone.

### Isaiah and his sons as signs (Isaiah 8:18)

Isaiah (Greek *Esaias*) comes from the Hebrew and means “God saves”, which was his great message. Isaiah’s 2 sons had names with meaning too, originally words to King Ahaz:

- **Shear-Jashub:** “a remnant shall return”
- **Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz:** “speed, plunder”

These sons must have been a constant reminder to Isaiah of the burden of his prophetic word: “Destruction and exile were coming … yet a remnant would return!”

### Orthodox Church calendar

The Prophet Isaiah is commemorated on 9th May. His prophecies are read in the daily readings of the Church throughout Great Lent and on the Eve of the Nativity. One of the most beautiful prophecies read on Christmas Eve is from Isaiah 9:6 and 7: “For unto us a child is born, unto us a Son is given.”

### Isaiah died the death of a martyr

Jewish tradition says Isaiah was killed by being sawn in two by King Manasseh, the son of King Hezekiah. Isaiah paid for condemning Manasseh’s idolatry. Orthodox Tradition says Hebrews 11:37 refers to Isaiah even if he isn’t named.

### Isaiah and Micah

- Isaiah and Micah worked in the southern kingdom of Judah during the period in which the neighbouring kingdom of Israel collapsed.
- Micah and Isaiah spoke out against the idolatry and hypocrisy of the people of Judah.
- Isaiah revealed God’s plan of salvation through his prophecies of the coming Messiah.

### Isaiah: the most quoted Old Testament prophet

Later stages of Israel’s and Judah’s history:

- During these 5 Kings of Judah: Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz (very bad), Hezekiah and Manasseh (very bad).
- Lived 700 or more years before Jesus was born.

Breakdown of Isaiah’s book:

- Key theme: Judgment and Salvation
- Chapters 1-39: the Book of Judgment
- Chapters 40-66: the Book of Comfort

### The Dead Sea Scrolls

- Discovered by a Bedouin in a cave near the north end of the Dead Sea.
- A complete manuscript of Isaiah, written in Hebrew about 100 BC, 1000 years earlier than the oldest known Hebrew text.
- Confirmed the remarkable accuracy of the book of Isaiah.

## Organising the big book of Isaiah

| Chapters | What Isaiah describes |
| --- | --- |
| 1-5 | The Lord’s complaints against Judah and Israel, the future glory of Zion and the coming day of the Lord |
| 6-8 | God calls Isaiah and commissions him for ministry |
| 9-12 | God will use the nations to punish the Israelites, who will ultimately be destroyed by the Assyrians; however, a remnant will return to the land and the Assyrians will be destroyed |
| 13-23 | Isaiah prophesies judgments against surrounding nations |
| 24-27 | Isaiah prophesies universal judgment and God’s ultimate triumph over evil |
| 28-35 | Isaiah prophesies against Ephraim, Jerusalem, Lebanon and the Assyrians; God speaks of the Messiah’s ministry, the day of the Lord and Israel’s restoration and glory in the kingdom |
| 36-39 | The siege of Jerusalem; God extends Hezekiah’s life; Babylonian exile is predicted |
| 40-48 | God’s comfort of his people after judgment, assuring them that God will deliver them |
| 49-57 | The Messiah’s obedience, mission, suffering and crucifixion; God assures the Israelites that He still loves them in spite of their sin and has not forsaken them |
| 58-66 | Israel’s sins, the consequences and the need for repentance; God’s future involvement with the Gentile nations and with Israel |

### Big ideas in Isaiah

- Earthly alliances disappoint, but trusting God brings salvation.
- Because God is holy and loving, he must punish the rebellious; but he will eventually redeem those who repent.
- God is the ultimate King who is just and righteous.
- God promises to send the Messiah, who will bring justice and salvation to all who believe.

### The message of Isaiah

The 3-point message of the Old Testament prophets is epitomised in the Book of Isaiah:

1. You (Israel/Judah) have broken the covenant; you had better repent!
2. No repentance? Then judgment! Judgment will also come on all nations.
3. Yet there is hope beyond the judgment, of a glorious future restoration for both Israel/Judah and for the nations.

The specific failings of the Hebrew people were especially idolatry, social injustice, and reliance on religious ritualism instead of true worship.

We in our turn wonder how prophecies that have not happened yet will be fulfilled. We don’t know when or how, but we can be sure that these prophecies will be fulfilled exactly as God foretold.
