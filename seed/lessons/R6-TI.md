## Artoklasia and Memorials
In addition to introducing the children to the practice of praying for the departed, teachers are also to recount stories from the Church’s Tradition which evidence the efficacy of this ancient practice. Several examples, reasons for the Church’s practice (as well as the practice’s antiquity), can be found in the images section for this session.

## The Epitaphios
In this session, the children will learn about the Epitaphios, what it is, and the occasions for its use. In addition to the material provided, the teachers could talk about how in processing with the Epitaphios, we are actually participating in the death and burial of Christ itself. See: [Mark 15 ESV - Jesus Delivered to Pilate - And as soon - Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2015&version=ESV).

## Confession
This session will cover what confession is (and isn’t) and why we do it. It also aims to give the children tools to critically reflect on their own lives, both in the past and going forward, and how Confession can be utilised by them in their repentance in order to become closer to Christ.

## Saint John of Damascus
With the documents for this session, the children will learn about this important Orthodox saint, including his life and his defence of iconography. Teachers can also talk about his contribution to the Church’s liturgical tradition – which includes his famous Paschal Canon which we sing every Pascha.

## Iconoclasm and the 7th Ecumenical Council
This session develops on the themes discussed in the previous lesson. In addition to the material provided, teachers are also encouraged to consult the following for a more in-depth analysis of the issues surrounding Iconoclasm: [The Theology of the Icon - St Andrews Encyclopaedia of Theology](https://www.saet.ac.uk/Christianity/TheologyoftheIcon#section4).

## Isaiah
In this session, attention is paid to the prophet Isaiah – in particular, to his prophecy of the Suffering Servant (which connects to themes discussed in previous sessions for the Torch-bearers).

## Parables
This session will introduce the children to the notion of a parable and gives them several examples from the New Testament. It is also worth discussing that each parable contains layers of meaning – there is a mystery always to be discovered for those willing to delve deeper through prayer: ‘He who has ears to hear, let him hear’ (Matthew 11:15).

## Parable of the Sheep and Goats
Building on the foundation laid in the previous lesson, this session involves a deeper exploration of a single parable. Things to think about will include what the parable reveals about the Last Judgement, how we are to act, how we demonstrate our faith to Christ and what that involves.
