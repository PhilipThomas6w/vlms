## Icon Screen (Iconostasis)

When we come to church, we come into the Kingdom of God, Heaven. The icons tell us that we are now especially close to all the saints in heaven. We are all together. The icon screen joins together those of us on earth to those who are in heaven.

The screen is called an icon screen because we hang icons on it. The screen divides the area of the Holy Altar from the main part of the Church (Nave).

In the Temple in Jerusalem the Holy of Holies was separated from the rest of the Temple by a curtain.

The doors in the centre of the icon screen are called ‘the Holy Doors’ or ‘the Royal Doors’, because the King of Kings, Jesus Christ, comes out through these doors as the Word, the Gospel, and to meet His people in the Communion. The side doors are called the deacons’ doors, because the deacons use them.

There is always an icon of Christ and the Mother of God on the screen. Christ is on the right of the Holy Doors, the Mother of God on the left. Usually there is the saint of the church on the screen. There can also be icons showing Christ’s life. There can be icons of other saints too.

During the Liturgy there are two processions out of the doors in the screen and through the Church which link the people to God. In the first one the Gospel Book, the Word of God, is carried. This is the Little Entrance. The second procession is called the Great Entry. This is when the bread and wine are taken to the Holy Table so that they can become the Body and Blood of Christ.

Heaven and Earth are divided by a very thin veil like the curtain you can see.

## Christ

Jesus Christ is the Son of God. God wants us to rise from the dead and live with Him in His kingdom for ever, so He came to earth. He was born as a human being, with Mary as His Mother. He is completely human, and completely God, both at the same time.

**In the icon:**

The icon of Christ is always to the right of the Holy Doors. There is always a halo with a cross in it behind His head, showing that He is God. The letters in the halo are God’s name – they mean ‘I am’. He usually wears a blue outer garment to represent His Divinity with an earth-red garment underneath to show that He is human too. Often, His right hand is blessing us and in His left hand is the Gospel Book. But there are many different icons of Christ.

## Mary The Mother of God, The Theotokos

The Mother of God is always to the left side of the Holy Doors. We sometimes call her the Theotokos. This means the God Bearer because she was a real mother that gave birth to God in His human form. She is our heavenly mother and always prays for us if we ask.

**In the icon:**

The Mother of God has Christ in her arms. Her hand is pointing to Christ. She is telling us that her Son is the Son of God. She always has an earth-red cloak. There are always stars on the hood and shoulders of her cloak. She is usually holding Jesus, showing Him to us.

## St John the Baptist

St John is called the Baptist because he baptised people. He told people that Jesus Christ, the Son of God, was coming and that they should be ready for Him. They needed to get ready in their hearts; by being sorry for the things they had done wrong and doing good things instead. He baptised them with water. This showed the people that they had started a new life. Now they were ready for Jesus.

**In the icon:**

St John is always shown with rather scruffy hair and wearing skins because he lived simply in the desert.

## The Icon of the Patron Saints of Our Church

The icon which shows the name of our Church is the 318 Holy Fathers of the First Œcumenical Council at Nicea. This is a very long name so we can call them “The Holy Fathers of Nicea”.

### The Holy Fathers of the First Oecumenical Council

The Emperor Constantine called a meeting of 318 Bishops in Nicea in the year 325 as there was an argument in the Church about *whether there was ever a time when the Son of God did not exist.* Arius could not believe that the Son of God had always been with the Father and the Holy Spirit. This means that the Son of God is one of the persons of the Holy Trinity: Father, Son and Holy Spirit: One God. The meeting proclaimed that Arius was wrong. The first part of the Creed we say in the Liturgy was written at this Council.

**In the icon:**

The Bishops are sitting round in a similar way as shown in the icon of Pentecost, to remind us that this is a second Pentecost. The throne with the open Gospel Book shows that Christ is present at the Council. Emperor Constantine is shown on the right side in the front row.

Sometimes St Athanasios, who was a deacon, is at the bottom of the icon keeping the record of what was being said. But in other icons Arius is in the dark at the bottom with his hands over his ears not wanting to listen.

Among the Bishops at this council were St Nicholas, St Spyridon, and St Sylvester, St Alexander.
