## Questions

- Can you recognise the icons of Christ, the Theotokos and St John the Baptist?
- Can you recognise these icons: the Nativity (birth) of Christ; the Theophany (baptism of Christ); the Last Supper; the Crucifixion; the Resurrection?
- What is happening in each icon?

## Christ

Jesus Christ is the Son of God. God wanted us to know what He is like, so He came to earth. He was born as a human being, with Mary as His Mother. He is completely human, and completely God, both at the same time.

**In the icon:**

Every icon of Christ has a halo behind his head, showing that He is God. The letters in the halo show God’s name – they mean ‘I am who I am’. His right hand is blessing us. In His left hand is the Gospel book. Gospel means ‘good news’ – the good news about Christ becoming a man.

## The Theotokos

Theotokos means ‘God bearer’. She was chosen by God to give birth to Jesus Christ, the Son of God. We call her the Mother of God, because she is the mother of Jesus Christ. She is also our mother in heaven, and always prays for us.

**In the icon:**

The Mother of God has Christ in her arms. Her hand is pointing to Christ. She is telling us that her Son is the Son of God. She always has a red cloak. There are always stars on the hood and shoulders of her cloak.

## St John the Baptist

St John is called the Baptist because he baptised people. He told people that Jesus Christ, the Son of God, was coming and that they should be ready for Him. They needed to get ready in their hearts; by being sorry for the things they had done wrong and doing good things instead. He baptised them with water. This showed the people that they had started a new life. Now they were ready for Jesus.

**In the icon:**

St John is always shown with scruffy hair and wearing skins because he lived rough in the desert.

## The Church Year

### The Nativity of the Lord, Christmas

God comes from heaven and takes the form of a human baby. He is completely God, and completely human, both at the same time.

**In the icon:**

Christ God is in the centre in the manger, and His Mother is close to the baby resting. The ox and donkey also worship – they represent the whole of the animal kingdom. Above, the angels sing praises. The angel on the right is telling the shepherds about Christ’s birth. On the left the wise men are coming on horseback. They are looking at the star which is guiding them. St Joseph, Mary’s husband, is thoughtful. A person, who looks like a shepherd, reminds him of Isaiah’s prophecy: “a virgin will conceive”. At the bottom of the icon the midwives are preparing to bath Christ after his birth.

### Theophany

This word means the ‘showing forth (or revealing) of God’. St John the Baptist had been teaching everyone about wrongdoing (sin) and repentance (turning away from wrongdoing and doing good instead). He baptised people in the river Jordan so that their wrongdoing was washed away. Jesus comes to St John to be baptised. He never did anything wrong, so He does not *need* to be baptised. But for the first time in history we see that God is one, and yet three – Father, Son and Holy Spirit. The Father says ‘this is my beloved Son in whom I am well pleased’. The Son is being baptised in the water. The Holy Spirit is seen in the shape of a dove.

**In the icon:**

Christ stands in the River Jordan. Above Him, the Holy Spirit is seen in the shape of a dove. St John stands on the bank, on the left. He is full of awe, he hardly dares to touch Christ, the Son of God. The angels watch on the right riverbank. They dare not touch Christ, so they have covered their hands.

![Icon of the Theophany: Christ stands in the dark river Jordan wearing only a white cloth, St John the Baptist on the left bank rests his hand on Christ’s head, and four angels with covered hands watch from the right bank, with rays of light coming down from the top of the icon](theophany.jpg)

### The Last Supper

The night before He died, Christ celebrated the Jewish feast of the Passover with the disciples in an upper room. He blessed the bread and wine. The disciples received the Body and Blood of Christ in the bread and the wine.

**In the icon:**

Christ is in the centre. The twelve disciples are sitting around. St John sits on his left. Judas, the one who betrayed him, is reaching out for food. On the table is the bread and the wine.

![Icon of the Last Supper: Christ sits at the centre of a long curved table with the twelve disciples on either side, one of them reaching across the table; the table holds a bowl, cups and candlesticks, with buildings behind](last-supper.jpg)

### The Crucifixion

There is no suffering or sorrow that we can feel that Christ has not already felt. Dying on a cross is the most painful death the Romans could think of. God shows us how much He loves us by sharing everything that can happen to human beings.

**In the icon:**

Christ is on the cross, greatly suffering. Mary, His Mother, is standing nearby. She is crying bitterly. Three women are supporting her. John, the disciple is by the foot of the cross. Behind him is St Longinus, the centurion who said: ‘Truly this man is the Son of God.’ Behind the cross are the walls of Jerusalem. Beneath the cross is a cave with a skull. Christ was crucified at Golgotha, which means ‘the place of the skull’. It is believed to be the place where Adam was buried.

![Icon of the Crucifixion: Christ on the cross above the walls of Jerusalem, the Mother of God and other women at the left, St John at the right with the centurion behind him, and a skull in the cave beneath the cross](crucifixion.jpg)

### The Resurrection

God is Life and gives us life. The first man, Adam, was with God at first and he had life. When he turned away from God he died and remained separated from God in a place we call hades. After Christ died on the Cross He rose from the dead. He went to hades and set Adam and everyone else free so that they could also rise and be with Him in the Kingdom of Heaven.

**In the icon:**

Christ is holding Adam’s hand, pulling him out of hades. He has just pulled Eve out, and both of them are very joyful and thankful. Around them are king David and king Solomon, and all the prophets. All have been pulled out of hades. At the bottom, under the earth, we see the broken-down gates of hades and the empty tombs. We often see the devil bound and falling into hell.
