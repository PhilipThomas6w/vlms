## Questions

- What happened at Jesus Christ’s betrayal and Crucifixion?
- Why is the Cross so important for Christians?

## The Crucifixion (Luke 22:39 – 23:56)

After the Last Supper Jesus took His Disciples to the Garden of Gethsemane to watch and pray. They waited a long time (the Disciples had even fallen asleep) before Judas came with soldiers to arrest Jesus.

Jesus was taken to be tried. His accusers twisted the truth, made fun of Him, spat on Him and whipped Him. He was condemned to death by crucifixion, the worst punishment. He was led out to a place called Golgotha, meaning the place of the skull.

The soldiers nailed Jesus to the cross and put an inscription above Him saying “The King of the Jews”.

Two thieves were crucified at the same time. The one on the right of Jesus believed that He was the Son of God and said “Remember me when you come into Your Kingdom”. Jesus replied “You will be in Paradise with Me today”.

Some people mocked Jesus. They said things like “He saved others, let us see if He can save Himself”.

Some people were shocked and sad. Mary, His mother, was there with other friends. Jesus gave His friend John to His mother as a son.

At the ninth hour, about 3 o’clock in the afternoon, Jesus cried out and died. When He died the earth shook, the sky darkened and the curtain of the Holy of Holies split in two. The centurion, Longinus, had been standing on guard by the cross. He suddenly realised that Jesus was truly God.

Joseph of Arimathea went to Pilate to ask if he could have Jesus’ body. His friends took Him down off the cross, wrapped Him in clean linen and laid Him in the new tomb which belonged to Joseph of Arimathea. The soldiers rolled a large stone in front of the tomb and put soldiers to guard it.

Because it was the Jewish feast of the Passover there was nothing more Jesus’ friends could do until the feast was over.

Although this was a terrible thing to have happened, we now think of the Cross as a sign of victory. Why?

## The Crucifixion and the Tree of Life

![Icon of the expulsion from Eden: Adam and Eve stand by the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil with the serpent coiled round it; a cherub guards the gate of Paradise; an angel drives the clothed Adam and Eve down the mountainside](expulsion-from-eden.png)

![Black and white drawing of Eden on a mountain, with Adam and Eve at the Tree of Life, and above it Christ crucified between the Mother of God and St John, with the words IC XC NI KA (Jesus Christ conquers), the sun and moon, and angels](tree-of-life-and-cross.png)

First, let us examine two representations of Eden, above. Eden means “Paradise”, that is, “the Garden”. In the midst of Eden we can find the *Tree of Life* and the *Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil* (Genesis 2:8). We also find four rivers flowing out of the centre of Eden (Genesis 2:10). Now, since the source of a river is generally found on top of a mountain, we may deduce that the centre of Eden can be found at the top of a mountain.

In the icon on the left, we can see the *Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil*. We see the serpent coiled around the tree, having persuaded Eve to eat the forbidden fruit. Adam and Eve are naked at this time. Later, we see them being banished from Eden by an Angel, and, if we look carefully, we will notice that they are being ushered down a mountain and that they are now fully clothed. The gate to Paradise is now guarded by a cherub.

Because they disobeyed God’s Law not to eat the fruit of the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, Adam and Eve were cursed by the Law: that is to say, they were condemned to die. Now, this seems like a very harsh punishment, but to understand why this was the case, we need to understand that the word “curse” means to “excommunicate” or to *condemn to an evil fate*. To be excommunicated means to be cut off from communication with someone or something, and, in this case, that someone was God, the very Source of life. What we should understand from this is that if we want to communicate with God, we need to obey His Law, or His commandments, and that if we disobey God, we cut ourselves off from the Source of life.

In the picture on the right, we see a different representation of Eden. Like in the first image, Paradise is represented as a place situated on top of a mountain. In this image, we can see the four rivers flowing out from the very centre of the mountain, where we find the Tree of Life. There is something very interesting about this picture, however. You will notice that Jesus on the Cross is superimposed on the Tree of Life: in other words, the Cross and the Tree exist in the same place and are, in effect, the same thing. The way we understand this as Orthodox Christians is to think of the Tree of Life as a *type* of Jesus on the Cross. By this, we mean that the Tree of Life *foreshadowed* the Crucifixion. It was a *sign* of what was to come, a kind of prophecy.

![Icon of the Crucifixion: Christ on a dark cross with angels above, the sun and moon beside the cross-bar, the Mother of God and women on the left, St John and the centurion on the right, and a skull in the cave beneath](crucifixion-icon-2.jpg)

Crucifixion was considered to be the most painful way of executing a person, and it was reserved only for those who had committed the very worst of crimes. Roman citizens could never be crucified, which is why St Paul, as a Roman citizen, was beheaded rather than crucified. Unlike St Paul, Jesus was not a Roman citizen.

Crucifixion was used as a form of execution long before the existence of the Roman Empire. Some think that it was first used by the Neo-Babylonians, while others think by the Persians. It is recorded as being used as a means of execution as early as 600 years before the birth of the Lord. Originally people were crucified on trees rather than on specially made crosses. The executioners would find a suitable tree and affix the person’s arms to the tree branches. This is one of the reasons why the cross was sometimes referred to as “the tree”.

Now, let us look at the icon of the Crucifixion. First of all, notice that the Crucifixion took place on a hill, or mountain. This mountain was known as “Golgotha”, which means “The Place of the Skull”. Notice that there is a skull at the bottom of the icon: this represents the skull of Adam. We are taught that Adam was buried beneath the very mountain that the Lord was crucified upon.

St John Chrysostom, the author of the Divine Liturgy, says, “Some say that there Adam had died and lay buried, and that Jesus set up His trophy over death in the place where death had begun its rule. He went forth carrying His cross as a trophy in opposition to the tyranny of death; and, as is customary with conquerors, He also carried on His shoulders the symbol of His victory.”

What does this mean? Well, it should be very clear to us now that the Tree of Life and Jesus’ death on the Cross are in fact the same thing: the Tree is the Cross, and the Life is Jesus (John 14:6). Jesus dying on the Cross is what gives us life. But how?

During the Divine Liturgy, we hear in the Troparion (a type of prayer) the words “Thou hast redeemed us from the curse of the Law by Thy precious Blood. By being nailed to the Cross and pierced with the spear, Thou hast poured immortality on mankind, O our Saviour, glory to Thee.”

It is by eating the fruit of the Tree of Life that we are redeemed (that is, saved) from the curse of the Law: the curse that afflicted Adam and Eve, which caused them to be excommunicated or “cut off” from the source of life, God. This “fruit” is the Eucharist; it is the Body and Blood of the Lord. The Eucharist heals the spiritual wounds that we suffer because of our sins. In time, it can make us like Jesus Himself, and if we become like Jesus, then we are resurrected through Him.

Now, before we finish, I want you to think of one more thing. God told Adam and Eve not to eat of the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil. This was because they were not sufficiently “grown-up” yet. They couldn’t possibly know all of the consequences that their actions would have, so it was in their best interests to obey God until they had matured. In other words, it wasn’t that God wanted to deprive them of any good thing, but that He wanted to protect them from their own bad choices, just as any loving parent would. Because they disobeyed God’s commandments, they became cursed. This had terrible consequences, not only for them, but for all of their descendants, including you and me.

However, the Father loved us so much that He chose not to abandon us, and so He sent His Only-Begotten Son here, to the world that He created for us to live in, to save us from the curse. Jesus showed us the only way by which we could be saved, that is through Him. He reminded us of two commandments of God that we must obey if we are to be able to communicate with God. These are:

- Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your mind, with all your soul, and with all your strength (known as “The Great Commandment”).
- Love your neighbour as yourself (sometimes known as “The Golden Rule”).

Jesus prayed to His Father constantly and always obeyed Him, and He loved us so much that He was willing to suffer terribly on the Cross to show us what love really means. The greatest expression of love, that is to say, the most loving thing that a person can ever do for another person, is to lay down one’s life for one’s friends (John 15:13), that is, to sacrifice ourselves for those whom we love. Jesus was not only willing to do this, but was willing to suffer the worst kind of public humiliation and the greatest act of injustice in history. By enduring this, He showed us just how much He loves us all.
