## The Holy Table

In addition to following the document for the session, make a connection between the placing of relics in the altar with the planting of a seed which will bear fruit. If possible, show the children the following video, starting from 3:24: [the consecration video](https://youtu.be/fM2eNz3XkWg?si=_rywJKuSUSHtJuow&t=204), as you go through the stages of the consecration ceremony.

## Pascha

This lesson aims to familiarise the children with the Paschal service of the Church. In addition to the relevant documents, also discuss the symbolism of leaving the side doors open (i.e. the empty tomb), of going from darkness to light, and of knocking down the doors of Hades. This last point can be linked to the latter half of Chrysostom's *Paschal Homily*, found here: [Paschal Homily - OrthodoxWiki](https://orthodoxwiki.org/Paschal_Homily).

## Prayers to the Mother of God

Whilst discussing the prayers from the documents for this session, attention should be drawn to the relationship between Mary's position in the hierarchy of created beings and the efficacy of her intercessions before her Son. For more on this, and for useful pictures, see the booklet *Beneath Thy Compassion* (especially the section entitled 'The God-Bearer'), kept in the lesson library.

## Saints Nicholas, Spyridon and Demetrios

One aim of this story driven session is to get the children to see how the holy ones of God live their lives, and how we are to emulate them. In addition to the resources provided, the following video can also be consulted: [Saint Spyridon the Wonderworker and Champion of Orthodoxy (youtube.com)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rQqI8jwsy_s). There is also a wordsearch with a few questions in the lesson, on St Nicholas.

## Saint Constantine

In addition to the document for the session, also mention the role that St Constantine occupied at the Council of Nicaea (325), and how he conducted himself. Even though he was the emperor, he condescended to kiss the feet of an elderly Egyptian monk who had been hamstrung during the Christian persecution. This, and other relevant information can be found in the chapter written by Leo Donald Davis, kept in the lesson library.

## Moses and the Tabernacle

There is already a lot of material to cover in the session's document. However, if you feel that your students are able, please also introduce them to some of the ideas in St Ephrem the Syrian's *Hymns on Paradise*. More specifically, use it to make the connection between the Tabernacle and the structure of the Garden of Eden (and also the layout of Orthodox churches today!). This text can be found in the church's library.

## Resurrection

Whilst discussing this topic, using the resources in the relevant folder, make sure to discuss the types in the Old Testament which pointed towards the Resurrection. Aidan Hart's *Festal Icons* has a useful chapter on this, with images too. See also: [Counting sheep – a catechism in the catacombs (shrewsburyorthodox.com)](https://shrewsburyorthodox.com/catechesis/counting-sheep-a-catechism-in-the-catacombs/).

## Feasts of the Mother of God

This session will build upon the knowledge already covered in the early session on the Mother of God. For further information about the various Marian hymns, and for relevant images, consult the booklet *Beneath Thy Compassion*, kept in the lesson library.
