## The Trisagion

“Trisagion” is a Greek word. It means “Thrice Holy”.

The Father is **Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal**.

The Son is **Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal**.

The Holy Spirit is **Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal**.

The prayers we use at the beginning of any worship, at home, in church or anywhere else are known as the “Trisagion Prayers”.

They are addressed to the Holy Trinity and are very important because they remind us that we are standing before God Almighty: Father, Son and Holy Spirit. They put us and our prayer into the right context. Once these prayers have been said we can continue our worship as appropriate.

The first prayer is addressed to the Holy Spirit so that our prayer may be true and effective. We then ask God to forgive our sins. The Lord’s Prayer, which Jesus taught His Apostles, known as the “Our Father”, is included in these first prayers.

Orthodox Christians know these prayers by heart.

## The Trisagion Prayers

**In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.**

**O Heavenly King and Comforter, the Spirit of Truth, Who art in all places and fillest all things, the Treasury of blessings and Giver of Life, come and abide in us. Cleanse us from impurity and of Thy goodness save our souls.**

**Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us.** (three times)

**Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever and unto the Ages of Ages. Amen.**

**Most Holy Trinity, have mercy on us. O Lord, cleanse us from our sins. O Master, pardon our iniquities. O Holy One, visit and heal our infirmities for Thy Name’s sake.**

**Lord, have mercy. Lord, have mercy. Lord, have mercy.**

**Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever and unto the Ages of Ages. Amen.**

**Our Father who art in heaven, hallowed be Thy Name; Thy kingdom come; Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.**
