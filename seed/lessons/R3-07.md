## Baptism and Chrismation

Just before Jesus Christ ascended into Heaven He said to His Disciples, “Go and teach all Nations, baptising them in the Name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (Matthew 28:19–20).

Baptism is the most important event in the life of an Orthodox Christian. It is when we become Christian.

Human beings are physical and spiritual. Baptism is our spiritual birth.

There are three parts to the Baptism service. (A long time ago, when pagans were becoming Christians, it took much longer to do the whole baptismal rite. Sometimes several years passed between the different parts.)

1. The Making of a Catechumen (a learner)
2. The Baptism
3. The Chrismation

### 1. The making of a Catechumen

1. First the person presented for Baptism is exorcised. This means that the devil and any evil are told to leave and get out.
2. Then the Catechumen, or their God Parent if they are a baby, promises that they believe in Christ and rejects the devil. They spit on the devil to show that they mean it.
3. They confirm their belief by saying the prayer known as “The Creed”.

### 2. The Baptism

a) The Priest blesses the water in the font with the sign of the Cross and then anoints the candidate and the water with oil for healing. Evil is recognised as a reality, and just as the gladiators covered themselves with oil so that their opponents could not hold onto them, so the one about to be baptised is also covered in oil.

b) The candidate is immersed into the water three times: once in the name of the Father, twice and of the Son, and a third time and of the Holy Spirit.

- Water is an instrument of death and destruction.
- Water cleanses and purifies.
- Water gives life. Just as Christ rose from the tomb, the newly baptised is pulled free like Adam and Eve out of their tomb and rises with Christ to the Heavenly Kingdom and Eternal life.

c) After the Baptism the newly baptised is given a white garment to wear to signify purity. In baptism we are freed from sin, so we are pure, because we die with Christ in the water and we rise with Christ in His Resurrection from the dead. They are given a cross to wear too, as they must take up their cross and follow Christ from now on.

### 3. The Chrismation

The Chrismation is the anointing with the oil of chrism.

a) The special chrism oil, made from olive and other aromatic oils and spices, is blessed by the Patriarch about every ten years and then added to the remainder of the unused chrism to ensure continuity. The Priest anoints the newly baptised on the forehead, eyes, nostrils, lips, ears, chest, hands, feet and back, between the shoulder blades, saying “The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit” each time. The imprint of the Holy Spirit is made on the baptised person.

b) There follows a prayer to ask the Holy Spirit to protect the new Christian eternally.

c) The Priest sprinkles the Christian with water and washes off the chrism, saying: “You are baptised, you are illumined, you are sanctified, you have received anointing with Holy Chrism. You are washed in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.”

### The Tonsure

The new Christian’s hair is cut in the form of a cross. This is a sign that they belong to God, just as the master cut the slave’s hair to show that he owned and was responsible for the slave.

### The Procession

Finally, the baptised person goes on a journey round the font three times. They are then led into the Church. These are the first steps on their way to Eternity.

## The Holy Mysteries (Sacraments)

*Everything in life can be a sacrament, filled with the Spirit of God by His Grace.* “Whatever you do, do all to the glory of God” (1 Corinthians 10:31).

Through the Holy Mysteries, known as sacraments, the Orthodox bear witness to the work of God’s Holy Spirit in all aspects of Christian life and upon all creation.

**Baptism.** Today, the sacrament of initiation into Christ and into Christ’s Church frequently occurs in infancy. The priest immerses the child in water three times: once in the name of the Father, once in the name of the Son, and once in the name of the Holy Spirit. Baptism symbolises burial (as the child is immersed in water) and resurrection and new birth in Christ (as the child is lifted out). In baptism, one is born into the kingdom of God through renewal in Christ, becoming a new creation.

**Chrismation.** This sacrament occurs immediately after Baptism. The priest anoints various parts of the body with holy oil (chrism), marking them with the sign of the cross and repeating “The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit.” Chrismation is a personal Pentecost: the descent of the Holy Spirit. Through chrismation, the person receives the gift of the Holy Spirit and is welcomed into the Body of Christ, His Church.

## What is needed for a Baptism

- **A God Parent.** If a baby is being baptised the God Parents are very important. They dedicate the child to God and surround their God Child with a Christian life at home and in the Church.
- **A Font.** The font symbolises the tomb and a womb.
- **Water.**
  - Water is an instrument of death and destruction.
  - Water cleanses and purifies.
  - Water gives life. Just as Christ rose from the tomb, the newly baptised is pulled free like Adam and Eve out of their graves and rises with Christ to the Heavenly Kingdom and Eternal life.
- **Olive Oil.** Evil is recognised as a reality, and just as the gladiators covered themselves with oil so that their opponents could not hold onto them, so the one about to be baptised is also covered in oil.
- **A Candle.** The candle reminds us that we are being rescued from darkness and evil to light and the knowledge of Christ.
- **Chrism oil and a brush to anoint with.** The special chrism oil, made from olive and other aromatic oils and spices, is blessed by the Patriarch about every ten years and then added to the remainder of the unused chrism to ensure continuity.
- **A White Garment.** This signifies purity. In baptism we are freed from sin, purified, because we die with Christ in the water and we rise with Christ in His Resurrection from the dead.
- **A neck cross.** The baptismal cross is usually worn by every Orthodox Christian to remind them that they must take up their cross and follow Christ.
- **Scissors.** The hair is cut at the tonsure. This is a sign that they belong to God, just as the master cut the slave’s hair to show that he owned and was responsible for the slave.
- **A Towel.** Towels are needed to dry the Christian after the baptism.
- **Soap and a small towel.** These are needed to wash the hands of those who have chrism and baptismal water on their hands.
