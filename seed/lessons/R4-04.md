## St Demetrios

A long time ago, instead of everyone going to football matches on a Saturday afternoon, or going bowling or to the cinema, the people flocked to the arena to watch the gladiators and the chariot races. They thought it was great fun and cheered loudly for their team. The gladiators pitched their fighting skills against wild animals, slaves and sometimes even Christians.

About 300 years after Jesus Christ there was a soldier called Demetrios living in the important city of Thessalonika. He was a handsome, brave soldier with courteous manners and a Christian believer. The Emperor Galerius Maximilian was so impressed by Demetrios that he promoted him to head of the armies of Thessaly.

After his promotion Demetrios no longer made it a secret that he was a Christian. He openly began to teach that there was only one God who made everything. He explained how the Word of God, Jesus Christ, became man, was crucified and buried as a man, but as God, resurrected to save mankind from his sins and grant him eternal life. Many Greeks in the city believed what Demetrios was saying and they destroyed the gods they worshipped. Important citizens in Thessalonika were becoming jealous of the influence Demetrios was having.

When Galerius Maximilian visited the city on his way back from a victorious battle he wanted to celebrate, but when he heard about Demetrios he was furious and tried to change his mind by threatening him with death. The Emperor hated Christians and had them killed if he heard the name even mentioned.

Of course, Demetrios would not change his mind. He was thrown into a dungeon under an old bath house, where he was saved from a deadly scorpion by making the sign of the cross.

Galerius had brought his favourite gladiator, Lyaeos, with him. He was a huge man, and skilled in the arena. He was drawing large crowds to the arena and challenging the Christians to fight him.

Now there was another fervent Christian young man, Nestor, who went to find Demetrios in prison to ask for his blessing to fight Lyaeos. Demetrios gave Nestor a blessing to take on the ferocious giant. The young man looked like a mere boy as he entered the arena to meet Lyaeos, calling on help from the God of Demetrios. However, as Lyaeos raised his arm to fend off Nestor's attack he was delivered a fatal blow. The crowd's roar for Lyaeos turned to screams of horror as they watched the famous gladiator slump to the ground. The Emperor immediately ordered that Nestor be executed and sent soldiers to Demetrios' cell where the brave soldier was stabbed to death. Another friend of St Demetrios, Loupos, was present when he died. Loupos removed Demetrios' ring, scarf and robe and dipped them into the martyr's blood. Later they performed miracles. But when Galerius heard what was going on, the angry Emperor ordered Loupos to be put to death as well. Other Christians buried Demetrios in the place he died.

### Miracles

After the death of Galerius Maximilian, the Christian ruler, Constantine, came to power. He had a soldier serving him named Leontion. Leontion was taken ill in Thessalonika and was in such pain he wanted to die. Although he was examined by many doctors none of them could find a cure. Leontion, knowing where St Demetrios was buried, went to his tomb. He was immediately healed. As a thanksgiving he paid for the church of St Demetrios to be built. When Leontion was sent to another part of the empire he wanted to take some of the saint's bones with him. St Demetrios appeared to Leontion and told him not to separate the bones, so he took the ring, robe and scarf. With these carried in a chest he and his companions safely crossed a flooding river.

St Demetrios is known as a Myrrh Gusher (myroblitis). A sweet-smelling liquid streamed from his wounds and was the source of many miracles. So much myrrh flowed that channels had to be made to drain the church.

## St Nicholas (died 340)

St Nicholas is the patron of children, sailors, prisoners and the Varangian Guard. He was born in Patara in AD 270, the only son of wealthy Christian parents who died when he was still young. After Nicholas's parents died he was brought up by his uncle, the bishop of Patara, who ordained him a priest. Nicholas was reluctantly consecrated bishop of Myra when he was only 30. The young bishop suffered under Emperor Diocletian's persecution of Christians. He was imprisoned, beaten and starved. He was eventually released when Constantine became the Emperor and allowed Christianity to be a recognised religion.

There are many stories of miracles associated with the saint. The most famous is the story about three daughters of a man who had lost all his money and was about to sell them as slaves as he could not afford to keep them, let alone find a dowry for good husbands for them. When St Nicholas heard about this he took a bag of gold from his own inheritance and tossed it through their window. He did not want his identity known. Not long afterwards the eldest daughter was married. This kind act was repeated for each daughter. However, a watch was kept after the second bag of gold was tossed through the window to find out who the generous benefactor might be. St Nicholas was discovered, and although he asked that it should be kept secret, it was not.

Three innocent prisoners were about to be put to death. St Nicholas quickly went to the scene and stopped the executioner. He reprimanded the governor until he confessed he had accepted a bribe to have them killed.

Another time when there was a famine St Nicholas asked the crews of the grain ships anchored in the harbour at Myra to unload some of the corn for his starving people. Despite the grain being bound for the Emperor in Constantinople St Nicholas assured the seamen that all would be well. Sure enough when the ships reached their destination they were still full of grain although two years' supply of food and enough to sow a new crop had been left in Myra.

St Nicholas was one of the Fathers at the First Ecumenical Council where the Orthodox were defending the Faith against the heretical belief of the Arians, that Christ was not completely God. Arius would not be quiet and listen so St Nicholas punched him on the jaw. St Nicholas was stripped of his Bishopric and imprisoned for his action. However, after three days Emperor Constantine had a dream of Jesus Christ and the Mother of God visiting him in prison. One was carrying his gospel book and one was carrying his bishop's stole. He was immediately restored to his former position.

St Nicholas is above all known for his secret generosity so it is not surprising that he continues to bless us through the sweet-smelling myrrh which still exudes from his bones and is given to pilgrims at his shrine in Bari, Italy. His relics were taken here by sailors and protected by the Varangian Guard after the Muslims conquered Myra in 1087.
