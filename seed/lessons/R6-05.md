## Iconoclasm

For about 120 years there was a furious debate in the Church—*should we* have Icons or not? This happened between the years 726 and 843 AD

1) Icons help us to learn about the Orthodox Faith eg. either by showing us an event in Christ’s Life or by reminding us of an aspect of our Faith. Use an icon to show this eg. Baptism of Christ, Anastasis etc.

2) Iconoclasts = “Icon breakers” objected to icons because they thought that:

a) Icons were against the 10 Commandments Exodus 20:4:

*You shall not make for yourself an idol or likeness of anything whatever is in heaven above and whatever is in earth beneath and whatever is in the waters beneath the earth.*

And because God has no form (Deuteronomy 4:12)

*And the Lord spoke to you from the midst of the fire. You heard the sound of words but you did not notice a likeness, only a voice.*

b) The time of Iconoclasm mid 8th century to mid 9th century (726-842) when the Eastern Roman Empire (Byzantine) was weak and some emperors believed that their armies were being beaten by the Moslems because God was punishing them for having icons.

3) However, the Iconodules = “Servants of Icons” pointed out that:

a) The Commandment in Exodus 20 was flatly contradicted by Exodus 25:18:

*And you shall make two Cherubim engraved in gold, and you shall position them at both sides of the mercy seat.* (i.e. Angels placed over the ark in the Temple) Make sure they know what a Cherubim is!! (an Angel, “6-winged and full of eyes”)

See also Numbers 21:8, Ezekiel 41:1, 18, 19 and Hebrews 9:1-5

b) They also pointed out that after the Incarnation the situation is entirely different to that of the Old Testament because now God does have form in the God/Man, Jesus Christ. AND if you say that Christ cannot be depicted you are saying that He is NOT human. (May be helpful to point out that it would have been possible to have photographed Christ, if Digital cameras had been invented in 1st century Palestine).

### The History of Iconoclasm
Iconoclasm started in 726 when the emperor Leo III (717 – 741) had his soldiers vandalise and remove the great Icon of Christ that was positioned above the gate of the imperial palace. This caused a riot. We do not know why Leo did this. He was already a successful general who had soundly beaten the Moslems in 717.

His son Constantine V was a more vigorous Iconoclast who called a Council in 754 (Hiera) that found in favour of Iconoclasm. Constantine persecuted iconodules several of whom were martyred e.g. St Stephen the Younger. He was prone to pulling out monastics beards and clawing their faces with red hot irons.

Constantine’s son Leo IV the Khazar (his mother was a Khazar i.e. a Jew) 775 – 780 was also an iconoclast. However, his wife the empress Eirene was not and when Leo died, she became regent for her son Constantine IV who was a child.

In 787 Eirene (and Constantine) called a Council that met in Nicea and found in favour of Icons. From 797 Eirene (797 - 802) reigned alone as Constantine IV had died (probably as a result of being blinded by his mother.)

Eirene was deposed by Nikephoros I (802 – 811) also an iconodule but he was defeated by the Bulgarians in a battle where he was killed.

The next emperor Michael I (811 – 813) was ineffective but an iconodule. He was succeeded by Leo V (813 - 820) who resumed iconoclasm for the reason expressed in 2b above. He was opposed to icons that were hung in churches where they could be venerated. Despite being an iconoclast he was murdered (unpleasantly) by the followers of his successor Michael II (820 – 829) who continued with iconoclasm. After Michael died of natural causes, he was succeeded by his 14year-old son, Theophilos (829 – 842) who was also an enthusiastic iconoclast and had several iconodules flogged to death. However, despite being an iconoclast he and his armies were severely beaten by the Moslems and this brought an end to the reason for iconoclasm. When he died of dysentery, he was succeeded by his baby son Michael III (842 – 867), Theophilos’s wife, Theodora, acted as regent.

Theodora called another Council which again found in favour of icons and they were restored on the first Sunday in Lent in 843. This event is commemorated to this day as the Sunday of Orthodoxy.

### What did the Iconoclasts do?

The iconoclasts pulled down portable icons and burnt them. Where icons were painted on a wall they were covered in lime wash paint. Where there were icon mosaics they hacked them off the wall and replaced them with mosaic crosses. This was once to be seen in the church of the Keemesis in Nicea and in Hagia Sophia in Constantinople.

### The Conclusion the Church came to was that icons were essential for two main reasons

1. They demonstrated that God had become Man- people had seen Jesus, the God /Man, and could paint pictures of Him.

2. By using Icons, the beliefs of the Christians could be explained.

**Iconoclasm is a heresy! We must have icons.**

**The first iconoclastic Emperors.** About 700 years after Jesus’s Resurrection, Leo III and Constantine V made Holy Icons illegal. They not only destroyed icons, but for 60 years they persecuted the Iconophiles, many of whom were monks.

**Irene of Athens (c. 752 to 803 AD).** When her iconoclastic husband, Emperor Leo IV, died, Irene became Empress as her son was only 9 years old. She called the 7th Ecumenical Council, which formally revived the veneration of Holy Icons to the Churches.

**Iconoclasm raises its ugly head again!** Unfortunately Iconoclasm broke out once more in AD 815, also by an Emperor Leo (V this time), and carried on for nearly 30 years until another Empress (Theodora), again after her husband Emperor Theophilus died, managed to restore the icons to the Churches for good, in Orthodox Churches at least.

**Orthodox calendar: celebrating icons**

*First Sunday of Lent (Triumph of Orthodoxy).* The theme of this Sunday since AD 843 has been that of the victory of the Iconophiles. On this Sunday we process with Holy Icons and declare our Orthodox Faith.

*Sunday of the Fathers of the 7th Ecumenical Council (Sunday on or after 11th October).* We commemorate the Fathers who condemned the Iconoclastic heresy in AD 787. Many later died as martyrs during the second assault of Iconoclasm.

*Tradition: Synaxarion (Lives of the Saints).* “It was not simply the veneration of the Holy Images that the Fathers defended but the very reality of the Incarnation of the Son of God.”
