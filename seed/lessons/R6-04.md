## St John Damascene: Orthodox torch-bearer

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Born | c. 676 AD in Damascus (Syria), capital of the Umayyad Empire |
| Family | Very well-known and wealthy Christians |
| Education | Classical Greek: theology, law, astronomy, geometry, music |
| Languages | Greek, Syriac and Arabic; he only preached and wrote in Greek |
| First job | Treasurer to the Muslim Caliph (inherited job from his father) |
| Monk | From c. 706 AD at St Sabas in the Judean desert near Jerusalem |
| Preacher | At a famous church in Jerusalem; called “the golden speaker” |
| Poet | Possibly the greatest liturgical poet of the Orthodox Church |
| Relics | Died c. 749; his tomb at Mar Saba is still a place of pilgrimage |

## The greatest defender of icons

John is also remembered for his role in the Iconoclastic controversy. When the Byzantine Emperor said all icons were to be destroyed, John supported the Patriarch in Constantinople and wrote very clearly why icons not only made churches beautiful, but declared the truth of the Incarnation.

## “Go into all the world and teach”

Although John didn’t travel far, what he wrote has. Soon after he died much of it was translated into other languages: first into Arabic, then old Slavonic, Latin and eventually English.

## His book, “On the Orthodox Faith”

In this book he gathered up and summarised the teaching from all of the Church Fathers before him. He was proud his book did not contain a single new idea. His genius was not his creativity but his ability to let the grand Christian Tradition speak through him.

## Pascha: the Feast of Feasts

Thanks to St John we have so many beautiful songs to sing at Pascha. He wrote the famous Paschal Canon we sing every year, over 1,000 years ago.

![Icon of Saint John of Damascus in a turban and green cloak, holding a scroll with the words “Today is the day of Resurrection”](icon-day-of-resurrection.jpg)

## Orthodox Church calendar

St John Damascene is celebrated on 4th December, when we sing:

**Troparion**

> “You are a guide of Orthodoxy, a teacher of true worship, a torch-bearer of the Church and the harp of the Spirit … Intercede before Christ God to save our souls.”

**Kontakion**

> “Come, O ye faithful, let us praise the hymn writer, the teacher of the Church, the defender of her doctrines: he took up the Lord’s Cross as a weapon and overcame heresies …”

![Icon of Saint John of Damascus with a grey beard and white turban, holding a scroll, beside a quotation: “Let us praise John of Damascus: the composer of hymns, teacher of the Church and defender of its doctrine.”](icon-with-hymn-quotation.jpg)

## A place, not a surname

“Damascene” is not really St John’s surname. It comes from the place he was born: Damascus (Syria).

## John describing the Trinity

John used the sun to help him describe and explain the Trinity:

> “The Father is the sun; the Son the rays; the Holy Spirit the heat.”

Distinctive, but all the same substance.

## Orthodox Tradition (the Synaxarion)

One of the most famous stories handed down to us about John concerns a miraculous healing. He was falsely accused of writing a letter betraying the Caliph, who then cut off his hand. John prayed before an icon of the Theotokos, fell asleep and woke finding his hand had been restored. In thanksgiving he had a silver hand made and then attached it to the icon, which is now in a monastery.

## The first Christian to write about Islam

John was the first Church Father to experience Islam first hand. In his work “On Heresies” his last chapter is on Islam. For John, Islam was obviously a Christian heresy, as it denied Christ being God, becoming man, dying on the Cross and the Resurrection, all basic to Orthodoxy.

## The spread of Islam and the Arab Empire

- Muhammad’s successors were called Caliphs.
- The first 4 Caliphs ruled from Medina in Arabia.
- The Umayyad Caliphs made their capital in Damascus, and the Arab Empire spread greatly during their rule.
- Large parts of the East Roman (Byzantine) Empire were conquered by them, and Arabic instead of Greek became the official language.

![Map of the Mediterranean and Middle East showing the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire outlined, with Rome, Constantinople, Antioch, Damascus, Jerusalem and Alexandria marked, and the Arab Empire shaded in three stages: to 632, expansion to 661 and expansion to 750](map-of-the-empires.jpg)

## Living in one, belonging to another

In many ways, although John lived in the Umayyad Muslim Empire all his life, he felt and acted like a citizen of the Byzantine Christian Empire, where they spoke Greek. Only after his death did “Arabic Christianity” develop and his work was then translated.

## Jerusalem and Mar Saba

John preached in Jerusalem, at the church of the Holy Sepulchre, and was a monk at the monastery of Mar Saba (St Sabas) near Jerusalem.
