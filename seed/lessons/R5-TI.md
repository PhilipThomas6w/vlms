## Prosphora and Proskomide
This session serves as the entry point into a significant topic of the Heralds’ curriculum: the liturgical aspect of our Church life. Although the children may know what occurs during the Liturgy, they may not know what goes on before it. In other words, how do we get to the point where we can receive the Eucharist? To answer this question, the children will be introduced to the ideas of Prosphora and the Proskomede service (or the ‘Service of Preparation’). It is worth drawing a comparison between the image of each portion of the Prosphora (representing the saints and Christ) and the icons of the Last Judgement, or All-Saints, wherein Christ enthroned is flanked by His many saints. Note also that as with the portion of bread set apart in the memory of the Mother of God, so too is Mary situated at the right hand of Christ. The children will also receive a recipe to make their own Prosphora at home – encourage them to bring it along to Church next time round!

## The Liturgy and the Mysteries
With the documents provided, the children are to be introduced to the “goings on” of the Liturgy – what is it that we are participating in during the service? St. John Chrysostom, in his *On the Priesthood*, gives us an excellent insight into the awesome event that we enter into – the excerpt is in the images for this week’s session.

## Saints John Chrysostom and Basil the Great
Continuing with the liturgical thread of the previous two sessions, this session aims to introduce the children to saints John Chrysostom and Basil the Great – to whom are attributed Divine Liturgies which are still used in the Church today. St John was introduced in the previous session, with a focus on his description of the liturgy. Teachers are free to develop that theme further if they feel it necessary – otherwise, a look at the *Paschal Homily* should be conducted (an image of the end of it is in the images folder for this lesson). This can then be compared with the Anaphora of St Basil found here: [Anaphora Prayer of St Basil the Great \| Orthocath](https://orthocath.wordpress.com/2010/03/22/anaphora-prayer-of-st-basil-the-great/) . Again, what is it that we’re doing, or joining in with during the Liturgy – nothing short of an ascent into Heaven, into the Eighth Day, the Day to come!

## Saint Justinian
In addition to the document for the session, the teacher can develop the role that St. Justinian played at the Council of Constantinople (553). Additionally, a discussion of his exploits for the Byzantine Empire, and his beneficence towards the Church (i.e. the building of churches and monasteries etc) should be mentioned. Relevant images can be found in the folder for this month’s session.

## The Prophet Elijah (Elias)
There are many worksheets and images to use for this month’s session. A broad overview, or a focus on one aspect of his life from the Old Testament, would help the children to complete the worksheets provided.

## Miracles
The teachers should focus on various passages from the New Testament which exhibit different types of miracles. John’s gospel should be especially consulted, with attention given to how Christ heals – i.e through speech, and touch. In this example (John 9), Christ places mud on the blind man’s eyes and he then sees. This is not just a healing, but a re-creation – it echoes Adam being formed from the clay of the earth by God! What does this then say about who Christ is!

## Feasts of the Lord
This session will introduce the children to a variety of the Church’s feasts which focus on various points in the Lord’s life. Attention to the corresponding icons for these feasts should be paid, in addition to using the written material for this month’s session.
