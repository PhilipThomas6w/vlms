## The 318 Holy Fathers of the First Oecumenical Council of Nicaea in 325 AD

The Patronal Feast is on the 7th Sunday of Pascha.

As more and more people became Christians, especially after it was no longer an illegal religion, it was necessary to explain the belief and to find words to express the Faith as accurately as possible.

Soon after the Emperor Constantine legalised Christianity there arose a dispute as to who Jesus was. Was He created by God? Or did He always exist with God the Father? Was there a time when the Son was not?

This would mean that Jesus Christ was not the Son of God as we think of Him, but like us or an angel. We could not sing *Christ is Risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and on those in the tombs bestowing life*. We would still be locked out of Heaven and evil would still have a hold on us.

A furious dispute raged in many cities over the Roman Empire. Arius, a priest in the city of Alexandria, had begun in 312 to preach that Jesus Christ was created by God the Father.

Was there a time when the Son of God did not exist?

The Orthodox Christians said: NEVER! He has been with the Father and the Holy Spirit for ever and ever, from ages to ages, before time was created and after the end of time. He is outside time. Because He is Love He became Man in time, but He was still God.

Arius and his followers said “No, this is impossible. How could God become man, be born and then die on the cross?”

The Orthodox Christians said “Because He IS God.”

Arius said that Jesus Christ was not completely God.

In the year 325 Emperor Constantine called a council of all the Bishops to work out what the Church had always believed was the truth. He was trying to calm the anger.

Among the 318 fathers at the Council were Constantine, Nicholas, Spyridon, Athanasios, Sylvester and Alexander.

Arius was found to be wrong.

After many days of discussion, the Orthodox put together a statement of belief. It started *We believe in One God…*

They won the argument but the dispute continued for about 400 years.

Arius was popular and it was easier to explain his theory. Many influential people supported Arius.

Not long afterwards, Arius was to be made a Bishop in Constantinople but on the way he visited a public lavatory where his bowels ruptured and he died.

This Council gave us the first part of the Orthodox prayer of Faith, known in English as the Creed: I believe in One God, Father… Son… and Holy Spirit…

## In the Icon

The Bishops are sitting round in a similar way to the icon of Pentecost, to remind us that this is a second Pentecost. The throne with the open Gospel Book shows that Christ is present at the Council. Emperor Constantine is shown on the right-hand side in the front row.

Sometimes St Athanasios, who was a deacon, is at the bottom of the icon keeping the record of what was being said. But in other icons Arius is in the dark at the bottom with his hands over his ears, not wanting to listen.

## The Orthodox believe

- That God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit, One God.
- There was NEVER a time when God as Father, Son and Holy Spirit did not exist and there NEVER will be.
- We cannot completely understand this because we are created human beings.

|---|---|
| He WAS created | There was NEVER a time when He was not |

## Troparion of the Holy Fathers (Tone 8)

O Christ our God, You are infinitely glorified,  
For You established our Fathers as radiant stars on earth,  
You led us to the true faith through them,  
O most merciful One, Glory to You.
