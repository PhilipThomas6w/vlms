## Questions

- Why are Bishops so important in the Orthodox Church?
- Where do you find the Bishop’s throne in the church?
- What special icon is above it?
- What happens in the services to show that the Bishop still has care for us even when he is not with us?

## The Bishop’s Throne

In Greek Churches the Bishop’s throne is always on the right side of the Church, and there may be a throne at the East End behind the Holy Table (the original position).

The Bishop is the successor of Christ’s 12 Apostles and is the earthly representative of Christ in the Church.

The Greek word *Episcopos* means overseer. He is the head of the local Church in his diocese.

A Bishop dedicates churches, consecrates Antimensia, gives the blessings for services to be held in the churches for which he is responsible, and appoints all those who will hold any position in them. He ordains Priests and Deacons to help him.

Even if the Bishop is not personally present (he cannot be in all the churches at once) the throne is still his seat, and no one else ever sits in it. It is censed as if he were there during the services.

When he is in the Church for the Liturgy he stays at the throne until the Entry with the Gospel. At this point in the service, he is collected by the priests in the procession (like the Emperor being collected from his palace).

The Priests and Deacons turn to him for a blessing before they do or say anything. Even the Readers and the Servers get their blessings from the Bishop.

He wears splendid vestments with a mitre shaped like a crown. He has a staff with a serpent handle on top to remind us of Moses’ staff which he used to divide the Red Sea. He blesses with two hands and because he is the chief teacher and enlightener he also uses lighted candles.

Although Bishops are responsible for a Diocese, they are all equal amongst themselves. There are Metropolitans, Archbishops and Patriarchs as head of a group of Bishops.

An icon of Christ the High Priest is often above the Bishop’s throne to show Christ’s presence there.
