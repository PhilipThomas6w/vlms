## The Trinity and the Creed

### St Andrei Rubliof’s Icon of the Hospitality of Abraham

There is a feast in the year, Pentecost, where the Church celebrates the Holy Trinity. This icon we are looking at is one of the icons we use for the feast; the other is Pentecost, which shows the Holy Spirit descending on the disciples. The Trinity icon is based on the following story from the Old Testament (Genesis 18:1-10):

> Then God appeared to him at the oak of Mamre, as he was sitting in the tent door during the noon hour. So he lifted his eyes and looked, and behold, three men stood before him; and when he saw them, he ran from the tent door to meet them, and bowed himself to the ground, and said, “O Lord, if I have now found grace in Your sight, do not pass by Your servant. Let water be brought, and let them wash Your feet, while You cool Yourselves under the tree. And I will bring bread for You to eat. After that You may pass by, inasmuch as You have come to Your servant.” They said, “Do as you have said.” So Abraham hurried into the tent to Sarah and said, “Quickly, make ready three measures of fine meal; knead it and make cakes.” Then Abraham ran to the herd, took a young calf, tender and good, gave it to his servant, and he hastened to prepare it. He also took butter and milk and the calf he prepared, and set it before them; and he stood by them under the tree as they ate. Then He said to him, “Where is Sarah your wife?” He replied, “Here, in the tent.” Again He said, “I will certainly return to you according to the time of life, and behold, Sarah your wife shall have a son.” (Sarah was listening in the tent door behind him.) (Genesis 18:1-10) SAAS.

![Icon of the Hospitality of Abraham, the Holy Trinity: three angels with halos and golden wings seated round a table with a chalice, one in pink and blue at the left, one in dark red and blue in the centre and one in green and blue at the right, with a building, a tree and a mountain behind them; the title reads “The Hospitality of Abraham”](icon-hospitality-of-abraham.jpg)

We noticed in verse three that Abraham speaks to the three people as though they are one. This is why the Church chose this event to illustrate God’s Trinitarian nature.

You noticed a lot of things in this icon that told us something about the Holy Trinity:

- The angels are in a circle, which represents God’s unity.
- Their heads are in a triangle, which shows that God is also three distinct but not separate Persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
- The two right angels looked towards the one on the left, so you thought the left angel probably represents the Father, who is the source of the Son and the Spirit. The Creed tells us that the Son is begotten of the Father, and that the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father.
- All the angels wear some blue, which represents heaven and therefore tell us that each person is God, is divine.
- The central angel wears an earthy red garment as well as a blue one, so He represents God the Son who became human or ‘earthy’ for us.
- The angel on the right wears green which is a symbol of life (green leaves show a tree is alive), so he represents the Holy Spirit, ‘the Lord the giver of life’ as the Creed says.
- You noticed that the circle is not closed, but opens towards us at the bottom. God’s love within the Trinity does not remain closed, but pours out towards us. How?
- You noticed that there is a calf’s head in the chalice. This is the food Abraham and Sarah offered the visitors. You said that this must represent Christ who is God the Son who became man and died for us, and whom we receive as Holy Communion in the Liturgy.
- You noticed that the lines of the footrests join in us, the viewers. So the icon shows the world seen from God’s eyes or perspective and not ours. It is as though God is looking at us through the icon.

## The Creed

Here is the Creed we say at each Liturgy:

*I believe in one God, Father Almighty, Maker heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.*

*And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten from the Father before all ages, Light of Light, true God of true God, begotten not made, being of one essence with the Father, through Him all things were made. For our sakes and for our salvation He came down from heaven, and was incarnate from the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary and became man. He was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate, and suffered and was buried; He rose again on the third day, in accordance with the Scriptures, and ascended into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father. He is coming again in glory to judge the living and the dead, and his kingdom will have no end.*

*And in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father, who together with the Father and the Son is worshipped and together glorified; who spoke through the Prophets. In one, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic Church. I confess one Baptism for the forgiveness of sins. I await the resurrection of the dead and the life of the age to come. Amen*
