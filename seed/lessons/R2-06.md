## Feasts and Fasts

The Greatest Feasts are Christmas and Pascha. We fast for 40 days before Christmas and we fast for 40 days before Pascha, plus Great Week, which is the week between Palm Sunday and Pascha.

**Wednesdays and Fridays are fast days all through the year, except in fast-free weeks.**

**Saturdays and Sundays are never strict fast days. Wine and oil are allowed even in the Great Fast.**

**We also fast before we receive Communion.**

When Orthodox Christians fast before Communion they don’t have anything to eat or drink from the evening before until after receiving Communion. If the Liturgy is in the evening they should not eat or drink for 6 hours before receiving Communion. If someone is having a baby, ill, old or very young it is necessary to be sensible. Communion is more important than the fasting. Children can usually keep a strict fast before Communion from the time they are 7 years old.

A Lenten Fast, before special Feasts, and also on Wednesdays and Fridays, is made up of days when Orthodox Christians do not eat certain foods. They follow the instructions used by the Orthodox Church for at least sixteen centuries.

**Orthodox Christians fast in order to train themselves to control their passions (anger, greed, pride) and to have self-discipline.** If they learn to say “no” to ice cream in the fast they are more likely to be able to say “no” when they are asked to do something they know to be wrong, such as stealing or cheating.

## What We Fast From

**During Lenten Fasts we fast from:**

- All animal products:
  - Meat
  - Eggs
  - Cheese and butter
  - Milk and all other dairy products

**We also fast from:**

- Fish
- Wine
- Oil

## What We Can Eat in the Fasts

We can eat things like:

- Fruit
- Nuts
- Olives
- Lentils and other pulses
- Vegetables
- Mushrooms

We can also eat creatures that don’t have any bones:

- Prawns
- Squid
- Octopus

## Feasting

**What do we eat at Christmas to celebrate Christ’s birth?**

- Beef, turkey or goose
- Yorkshire pudding (made with milk, eggs and flour)
- Christmas pudding and brandy butter
- Biscuits and cheese

**What do we eat at Pascha to celebrate Christ’s Resurrection?**

- Lamb, sausages, chicken or pork
- Red eggs
- Pascha (the cake), made with cream, butter, cream cheese and sugar

## Prayer Before Meals

We should remember God is with us all the time. One way to do this is to always ask God to bless our food before we eat. It is necessary for us all to know a prayer by heart. We are often not at home, so even at school or with friends or non-Orthodox relatives we should pray silently to ask God to bless our food.

This is a simple prayer to bless the food:

> In the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. Amen.
>
> Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
>
> Lord have mercy, Lord have mercy, Lord have mercy.
>
> Christ God, bless the food and drink of Thy servants, for Thou art holy, both now and for ever and unto the ages of ages. Amen.
