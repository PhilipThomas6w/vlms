## The Liturgy of the Word

The word “Liturgy” means work. This is in the sense that when all the parts of a car or a clock are working properly it fulfils its proper function. Something happens, because the effort and energy of everything working together achieves this. The people at the Liturgy are joined to the work of Christ.

The first part of the Liturgy is called the Liturgy of the Word, though sometimes it is called the Liturgy of the Catechumens (which means learners – they are listening to the readings so that they may learn). It is in this first part of the Liturgy that the Bible is read.

The Gospel Book is brought out into the church and this represents Christ coming to be with His people.

It is then censed with incense, showing that God is present and then it is read. Lights (candles) are always used to show that Christ is the Light of the World.

The word Epistle means Letter, Apostolos means Apostle (because this was the book written by the Apostle Paul (mostly!). Evangelos means Good News. And so does the word Gospel.

When we come into the church we are coming into God’s house. We greet Him when we kiss the icons just as we greet our grandparents. Instead of saying “Hello” we could say “Glory to Thee O God”. At the beginning of the Liturgy the priest kisses the Holy Table and the Gospel. He lifts the Gospel and makes the sign of the cross while he says: “Blessed is the Kingdom of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever unto the ages of ages.”

At this moment it is as if we step over the threshold into heaven. Heaven and Earth are joined during the Liturgy. It is an exciting moment, the visible and the invisible are here – the angels and the saints and all the people – priests, servers, old, young, babies, singers – alive in the world or alive in heaven. We have been invited to a Heavenly Banquet.

We pray for peace and for all human needs, we respond with “Lord have mercy” (Kyrie eleison. The Greek word eleison, as well as mercy, means almsgiving and is related to the word for oil – so it means “sooth me, comfort me, take away pain, show me your steadfast love and compassion”). We chant psalms with refrains between the verses: “Through the prayers of the Mother of God, save us O Savior” and then “O Son of God who art risen from the dead save us who sing to Thee, alleluia”.

“Only begotten Son and Word of God” sums up what we believe.

Jesus Christ is present in His Gospel and He speaks to us through the words of His Gospel, so we honour the Gospel as if it were Jesus Christ Himself. At the Little Entrance, the priest takes up the Gospel book from the Holy Table and gives it to the deacon if there is one. Servers with candles go in front to show that this is an important moment. Christ is the one who leads us from darkness into Light, so that we can see with our physical eyes and our spiritual eyes. We bow and make the sign of the cross. It is as if Christ is coming from Heaven into the World. In ancient times this was when the Emperor, Patriarch, bishops, priests, Varangian Guard and all the courtiers and people would arrive at church.

The priest asks God that the angels may enter with us and be with us as we honour and worship Father Son and Holy Spirit. He places the Gospel onto the Holy Table – Christ now sits on the throne. We really are in Heaven and we sing the hymn of the angels with the angels “Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal One have mercy on us.”

A reader reads the Epistle (Apostolos), a letter written by an apostle to teach the Christians the truth about God. Before the Gospel is read it is censed from four sides, to show that it is Jesus Christ, the Son of God, here with us. This is why everyone stands and the clergy remove their hats. Again, candles are brought to remind us that this is Christ – we are listening to our Lord Himself.

## The Liturgy of the Faithful

The Liturgy of the Faithful is what we have been preparing for. The gifts of bread and wine are carried through the church in a procession with candles, incense and the exapteriga, round icons of six winged angels on poles, and placed on the Holy Table. Everyone stands, bows and makes the sign of the cross as they pass to show their respect. The congregation are just like the holy angels, which surround God wherever He goes, as the hymn being sung tells.

After the Creed, or symbol of Faith has been proclaimed by everyone it is time for “God to act”. The priest remembers all that Jesus Christ has done for us especially at the Last Supper and using Christ’s very same words, asks that ‘the Holy Spirit will come down on us and on the bread and wine changing’ them to be His Body and Blood. The whole congregation bows deeply saying “Amen, Amen, Amen”. We are saying “it is definitely so, it is definitely so, it is definitely so”.

The prayer Our Lord gave to the disciples is said by the whole assembly. It is still known as the “Lord’s Prayer”. Then everyone is invited to share in the Mystic Feast which changes, them as it changes the bread and wine. The people, when they receive Communion, are filled with the Holiness and Life of God. It is a mystery! If we could see, perhaps each person would be shining as brightly as Christ did at the Transfiguration on the mountain.

Finally, the joyful hymns in thanksgiving are sung.

## Saint John Chrysostom on the Liturgy

> For the priestly office is indeed discharged on earth, but it ranks amongst heavenly ordinances; and very naturally so: for neither man, nor angel, nor archangel, nor any other created power, but the Paraclete Himself, instituted this vocation, and persuaded men while still abiding in the flesh to represent the ministry of angels. Wherefore the consecrated priest ought to be as pure as if he were standing in the heavens themselves in the midst of those powers. Fearful, indeed, and of most awful import, were the things which were used before the dispensation of grace, as the bells, the pomegranates, the stones on the breastplate and on the ephod, the girdle, the mitre, the long robe, the plate of gold, the holy of holies, the deep silence within. But if any one should examine the things which belong to the dispensation of grace, he will find that, small as they are, yet are they fearful and full of awe, and that what was spoken concerning the law is true in this case also, that "what has been made glorious hath no glory in this respect by reason of the glory which excelleth." For when thou seest the Lord sacrificed, and laid upon the altar, and the priest standing and praying over the victim, and all the worshippers empurpled with that precious blood, canst thou then think that thou art still amongst men, and standing upon the earth? Art thou not, on the contrary, straightway translated to Heaven, and casting out every carnal thought from the soul, dost thou not with disembodied spirit and pure reason contemplate the things which are in Heaven? Oh! what a marvel! what love of God to man! He who sitteth on high with the Father is at that hour held in the hands of all, and gives Himself to those who are willing to embrace and grasp Him. And this all do through the eyes of faith! Do these things seem to you fit to be despised, or such as to make it possible for any one to be uplifted against them?
>
> Would you also learn from another miracle the exceeding sanctity of this office? Picture Elijah and the vast multitude standing around him, and the sacrifice laid upon the altar of stones, and all the rest of the people hushed into a deep silence while the prophet alone offers up prayer: then the sudden rush of fire from Heaven upon the sacrifice:--these are marvellous things, charged with terror. Now then pass from this scene to the rites which are celebrated in the present day; they are not only marvellous to behold, but transcendent in terror. There stands the priest, not bringing down fire from Heaven, but the Holy Spirit: and he makes prolonged supplication, not that some flame sent down from on high may consume the offerings, but that grace descending on the sacrifice may thereby enlighten the souls of all, and render them more refulgent than silver purified by fire. Who can despise this most awful mystery, unless he is stark mad and senseless? Or do you not know that no human soul could have endured that fire in the sacrifice, but all would have been utterly consumed, had not the assistance of God's grace been great.
>
> For if any one will consider how great a thing it is for one, being a man, and compassed with flesh and blood, to be enabled to draw nigh to that blessed and pure nature, he will then clearly see what great honor the grace of the Spirit has vouchsafed to priests; since by their agency these rites are celebrated, and others nowise inferior to these both in respect of our dignity and our salvation.

St John Chrysostom, *On the Priesthood*, Book III, 4–5, translated by W. R. W. Stephens, in *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, First Series, Volume 9, edited by Philip Schaff (1889); text from the Christian Classics Ethereal Library ([ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf109](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf109)).
