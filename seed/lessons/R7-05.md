## St Kyril and St Methodios and the Mission to the Slavs

The Byzantine Christian Empire was constantly being attacked by Arabs and Barbarians. Over the centuries there were movements of people, even whole tribes, as they sought better climates and new pastures for their herds and flocks.

Barbarians from Asia invaded what we now think of as Europe, creating new kingdoms of Visigoths, Ostrogoths, Franks and Vandals. The Slavs were the Barbarians of Eastern Europe. They were pressed by an invasion of Huns, and became known as Czechs, Moravians and Poles. They in their turn were invaded by a Mongolian tribe, the Bulgars. Gradually the Bulgars mixed in with the Slavs and acquired the Slavic language. Unlike the ancient civilised world of Greece and Rome, they were uncivilised, pagan or heathen and were always on the move, continually at war with each other.

However, little by little, Christian missionaries moved among these people and pockets of Christianity were established.

One of these was in Moravia. In 862 AD Prince Rastislav of Moravia asked Emperor Michael III of Byzantium to send Christian missionaries to preach to his people. He wanted the Services and the Scriptures in their own language so that they would be able to understand. Up until this time Greek, Latin and Syrian only had been used in Church.

The obvious choice was to send the two famous brothers, Kyril and Methodios. They could speak the Slavic language as they had been brought up in Salonica (Thessaloniki) where many Slavs were living. They had also been on a missionary expedition previously. This time, though, there was a problem. No one had ever written anything down in Slavic and Kyril and Methodios would not go to Moravia without books.

Kyril and Methodios set about working out a Slavic alphabet and started to write down the Church Services and Scriptures.

So a year later the envoys from Byzantium arrived with an amazing gift for Prince Rastislav. They brought books written in his Slavic language. Can you imagine such a present?

The missionaries established schools, built churches, and trained priests. People flocked to hear them preach. Vast new territories were converted to Christianity. However, it was not without problems and eventually those who insisted the only church languages that could be used were Latin and Greek influenced the Moravians and they returned to the use of Latin.

In the meantime, Prince Boris of neighbouring Bulgaria had realised the importance of Christianity and wanted to create a great Christian country for himself and his people. After Kyril and Methodios died (869 and 885), their disciples went on to Bulgaria using the Slavonic texts.

This influence then spread to Serbia and was later adopted by Princess Olga and her grandson, Prince Vladimir of Kiev. Hence Russia also gave up barbaric ways and became civilised Christians.

Slavonic is still the language that the Slavic nations use for their services. The Glagolitic alphabet that Kyril first devised was simplified and modified and is now known as the “Cyrillic” script. St Kyril and St Methodios are called Equal to the Apostles and Enlighteners of the Slavs, or Apostles to the Slavs. Their feast day is 11th May.

## Round Up: Saints Kyril and Methodios and the Mission to the Slavs

The Christian Empire was constantly being attacked by Arabs and barbarians.

When Prince Rastislav of Moravia, a barbarian state, asked the Byzantine Emperor to send him priests to teach his people about Christianity, the Byzantines, hoping for peace, took this very seriously. They sent a competent envoy of missionary priests. The most famous were Kyril and Methodios, two brothers brought up in Salonica (now Thessaloniki), where at that time both the Slavic language and Greek were spoken. But Slavic was not a written language. Kyril and Methodios set about devising an alphabet for the Slavic sounds.

On arriving in Moravia, Kyril and Methodios gave Prince Rastislav an amazing gift: books written in his own language, especially books for celebrating the church services and the Holy Scriptures. Can you imagine such a present? It meant that the Prince and his people could become Christians and learn and understand their Faith.

Christianity quickly spread to neighbouring Slavic-speaking states: Bulgaria, Serbia and Russia.

Later the alphabet was simplified and called “Cyrillic” after St Kyril. It is still used and services are still sung in the old Slavonic language.
