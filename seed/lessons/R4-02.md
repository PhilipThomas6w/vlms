Words to know:

- **Jesus** = Christ, Son of God, Son of Man, Messiah, Lord
- **Adam** = Man
- **Eve** = Woman
- **Devil** = Satan, the Evil One
- **Man** = mankind (not animal)
- **Incarnate** = made flesh, made man
- **The Holy Trinity** = God the Father, God the Son, God the Holy Spirit (God is Love, so there must be three persons of the Trinity to love each other, because otherwise Love would be selfish)

### Pascha: the Feast of feasts

Pascha celebrates Christ's resurrection and (because of our baptism into Christ) our resurrection too. This is the climax of all the Church Feasts. The 12 Great Feasts of the Church year prepare us for this one Feast of feasts.

### St Gregory on Pascha

"Pascha is the Feast of feasts, like the Sun above the stars." Pascha is the centre around which the 12 Great Church Feasts revolve.

### Holy Week and Pascha

Pascha and Holy Week go together! Good Friday and Holy Saturday are very much part of the Pascha story. Put another way, Christ's Crucifixion and Christ's Resurrection are two sides of the same coin.

### From Passover to Pascha

Our word Pascha comes from the Hebrew word *pesach*, meaning passover or passage. Early Christians took the Jewish (Old Testament) Feast of Passover celebrating the Exodus, and transformed it into a Christian Feast celebrating "Christ our Pascha", our passing over from death to Life.

### St Paul on "Christ our Passover"

St Paul writes, “Christ, our Passover, was sacrificed for us.” (1 Corinthians 5:7) NKJV. He saw Christ as the new Passover delivering not one tribe but everyone from slavery: not from an earthly Pharaoh, but from a universal tormentor (Satan), and the power of sin and death.

### Pascha and Baptism

Everything about Pascha night reminds us of our Baptism: the use of candles, white robes and so on. And instead of the Trisagion we sing the baptismal hymn: "As many as have been baptised into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia!"

### The Paschal Gospel reading

The first 17 verses of St John's Gospel are read at the midnight Liturgy. They speak of Christ as "The true Light Who has overcome the darkness and Who gives new life to all who will receive Him." Like the book of Genesis in the Old Testament, it starts with the words: "In the beginning..."

### The Paschal Prokeimenon

This verse speaks of a new day of creation: "This is the day which the Lord has made! Let us rejoice and be glad in it (and Him!)." Psalm 117 (118):24

### Psalm 67 (68): "Let God arise"

King David spoke of the power of the Resurrection when he wrote, "Let God arise; let His enemies be scattered." This psalm begins all of the Church services during the Paschal season.

### Paschal hymn to the Theotokos

> Shine! Shine! O New Jerusalem!\
> The glory of the Lord has shone on thee.\
> Exult now and be glad, O Sion.\
> Be radiant, O pure Theotokos,\
> In the resurrection of thy Son.

### Paschal Canon: first song

> This is the day of resurrection.\
> Let us be illumined, O people.\
> Pascha, the Pascha of the Lord.\
> For from death to life\
> And from earth to heaven\
> Christ our God has led us,\
> As we sing the song of victory.

## The Paschal Season and Traditions

*From the group's Pascha handout.*

### The Paschal season

The Paschal season lasts for 40 days! It ends with the Feast of the Ascension. The Paschal troparion is sung, and the Paschal greeting exchanged, so many times that the truth "Christ is Risen" rings in our ears!

### The Paschal greeting

During the Paschal season, Orthodox Christians greet each other in a special way. Instead of the usual "hello" or "hi", one person says "Christ is risen!" and the other joyfully replies, "He is risen, indeed!"

### The Paschal greeting in Chinese

Orthodox Christians often recite the Paschal greeting in different languages in church, as an act of Orthodox unity across languages and cultures. Fr Stephen loves saying it in Chinese!

### Breakfast at midnight

At the end of the midnight Liturgy, the priest gives everyone a hard-boiled egg to eat for breakfast! After fasting from eggs during Lent, they taste especially good!

### The Pascha basket

People often bring baskets of food to the front of the church for the priest to bless for the midnight Paschal feast. They are full of food not eaten during the Lenten fast: meat, cheese, butter and eggs, including chocolate ones!

### Decorating eggs

Apart from just dyeing eggs red, some Orthodox paint beautiful patterns in many colours or Christian symbols, like the Cross, on the eggs.

### Tapping eggs

This is a fun tradition where 2 people gently tap their eggs together. The winner is the one whose egg hasn't cracked. Make sure the egg is hard-boiled!

### The colours of Pascha

Red and white are very much in evidence at Pascha! The priests' vestments, and all the many flowers decorating the church are bright red and white. After the dark colours of Lent everything looks especially beautiful and full of light and joy!

### "Come receive the light"

Inspired by the Jerusalem tradition of the Holy Fire/Light, the priest lights the Trikirion (3 branched candlestick) from the vigil lamp on the altar and then that lights all!

### Processional hymn

> Thy Resurrection, O Christ our Saviour,\
> The angels in heaven sing.\
> Enable us on earth\
> To glorify Thee in purity of heart.

### The Paschal hymn

*(A shout of triumph)*

> Christ is risen from the dead,\
> Trampling down death by death,\
> And upon those in the tombs\
> Bestowing life.

### The Paschal Canon

After the midnight procession, when we are all back in the church, the whole church bursts into song. The words we sing were composed by St John of Damascus over a thousand years ago! Again and again we sing of the joy of the Resurrection.

### The famous Paschal Homily

Every Pascha night the sermon St John Chrysostom wrote in the 4th century is read by the priest. It invites all to the feast whether they have kept the fast or not. It tells how God's love and mercy is for everyone.

### The raising of the Cross

During the Paschal vigil, the priest raises the Cross and sings: "Christ is risen!" "For BY THE CROSS, Christ has conquered death and sin which held us captive."
