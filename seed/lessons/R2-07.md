## Moses in Egypt

The Hebrew people were very upset. Many years ago their great, great, grandparents had gone to Egypt to live. At first they were made welcome but then Pharaoh, the King of Egypt, made a law that every boy baby of the Hebrew race should be killed, and there was great sadness because of it. But when Moses was born, his mother managed to hide him for three months; she made a basket into a cradle and covered it with tar to make it waterproof and, putting him into it, carried him down to a river and hid the cradle among the reeds there.

Soon after this, Pharaoh’s daughter came with her maidens to the river-side, and when she saw the beautiful child, she sent one of her maidens to bring it to her. She took the little boy to the palace and named him Moses, and he became a great man among the Egyptians. He knew, however, that he belonged to the Hebrew race, and when he saw how badly his own people were treated, he tried to help them; he killed an Egyptian when he thought no-one was looking. That meant he had to leave Egypt, and he became a shepherd, taking care of the flocks of a priest called Jethro. He also married Jethro’s daughter.

After a time, when Moses was in the wilderness, God spoke to Moses out of a burning bush, and told him that he must go and rescue his people from the cruel Egyptians. Moses thought he could not do this; but God promised to help him.

God said that Moses could take Aaron, his brother, with him as Moses was not very good at speaking out loud to important people.

Moses and Aaron went to Pharaoh, and told him that it was the Lord’s command that he should let the Israelites go. Pharaoh knew nothing about God, and became very angry.

God then sent lots of plagues onto the people of Egypt. Moses turned the waters of the rivers into blood; and after that he caused large numbers of frogs to run over the land and through the houses, doing great harm. He also brought locusts and other insects to be a pest to the people, and caused many of the useful animals which belonged to the Egyptians to grow sick and die. There was then a big storm with huge hail stones. But Pharaoh would not listen to him.

At last, however, God sent more terrible trouble; for the first-born of every Egyptian family, and even the first-born among their flocks, died; although the Israelites, who were constantly praying to the Lord and making sacrifices, were spared, as they had been all the time. The Israelites were told to put blood from a lamb around the door so that their first-born would not die.

Then Pharaoh was frightened into obeying God, and he let the Israelites go; so they started at once for the land of Canaan, and the Lord guided them by a cloud, which at night looked like a pillar of fire.

When the Israelites had reached the Red Sea, they found that Pharaoh was chasing them with a large army. But God commanded Moses to stretch forth his rod over the sea; he did so, and the waters parted, making a high wall upon either side, so that the children of Israel passed through and reached the other side in safety. Pharaoh and his army followed and were all drowned.

After they had travelled for some time, they were in need of bread and meat, and they complained about Moses because he had brought them to a land where they had not enough to eat. But God sent them plenty of quails (a very small bird) and manna (bread) which fell from the sky.

## Moses and the Burning Bush

Moses had run away from Egypt because he had killed an Egyptian who was being cruel to a Hebrew slave. He went into the desert where no one would follow him. Only shepherds and their families lived there, wandering with their flocks wherever they could find grazing.

One day he was resting by a well when seven sisters came to collect water. A group of shepherds arrived to get water and roughly pushed them out of the way so that they could get the water first.

Moses leapt to his feet and drove the shepherds from the well. He helped the girls fill their jars and give water to their sheep.

They were so grateful that they took Moses back to their father, Jethro’s tent. Jethro invited Moses to live with them. Moses married his daughter, Zipporah.

Sometime later, as Moses tended his flock in the desert he came to Horeb, the mountain of God. Here he noticed that a bush had caught fire. Flames crackled along the branches, but the bush was not burnt up. Moses went closer to see why.

A voice spoke to him, “Moses!” It seemed to be coming out of the fire.

“Here I am,” he answered.

“Don’t come any closer! Take off your sandals! This is holy ground. I am God!”

Moses hid his head, afraid. He was even more frightened when he heard God say, “I am sending you to the king of Egypt to rescue the Hebrew people from slavery.”

He began to make excuses.

“No one will believe me. I’m a nobody. I can’t even speak properly!”

“I will go with you,” God promised. “When the Egyptian priests try their magic on you, I’ll help you do even more wonderful things. I’ll tell you what to say.”

“What if they ask for a name?”

“Tell them ‘I AM THE EXISTING ONE’ has sent you,” replied God. “This is My name for ever.”

“Please send someone else,” Moses begged.

God grew angry. “I have chosen you, but I will send your brother Aaron to meet you. He will speak for you, and I will help you both.”

Moses set off back to Egypt to do as God asked him.
