## The Trisagion

In addition to progressing through the document in the folder, and working with the children through each part of the prayers, it is worth noting that the earliest recorded instance of the Trisagion prayers occurs in the Acts of Chalcedon, where it is said by all of the bishops in unison. This locates it as early as 451! It is also worth mentioning how calling God thrice-holy is also how the angels chant hymns of praise near the throne of God (Isaiah 6:3).

## The Early Church

This session is to introduce the children to the emergence of the Christian Church. Emphasis should be made on the apostolic feature of the Church’s growth. This will also introduce them to the idea of apostolic succession. For example, saints Polycarp and Ignatius that were covered earlier were bishops in Smyrna and Antioch respectively, and both of them were disciples of St. John the Evangelist.

## The Patronal Icon of our Church

This session serves as a gentle introduction to the initial Arian controversy, and the Council of Nicaea that followed it. It is important to convey that bishops from all over the world (from churches which can trace their lineage back to the apostles – which they learned from the previous session) met and agreed that Christ was the Son of God, Who was Himself divine. This wasn’t just an intellectual debate. The Nicenes defended Christ’s divinity because it was what they experienced in the liturgy, and had long since hymned about.

For example, see the Oxyrhynchus Hymn here: [chantblog: The Oxyrhynchus Hymn](https://chantblog.blogspot.com/2011/03/oxyrhynchus-hymn.html)

## Saint Paul

For this session, explore who Paul was before his experience on the road to Damascus, such as his involvement in the death of St. Stephen. Then discuss his “conversion” experience, and who he then became as a result. Does this have any parallel to our own lives? Then, mention his travels with Barnabas, using the map to illustrate.
