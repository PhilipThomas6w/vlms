## Emperor Constantine the Great (273-337 AD)

The Roman Emperor, Diocletian, divided the Empire into 4 parts. Constantine's father, Constantius Chlorus, was governor and co-emperor of Gaul (what is now Britain and France and Spain). Constantius' wife, Helen, and many of the Empire's soldiers were Christians. The other co-emperors, Diocletian, Maxentius and Galerius, cruelly persecuted the Christians.

Constantine was an officer in the army and his travels took him to the palace of Diocletian in Nicomedia. It was here that he witnessed many persecutions against the Christians. Many of our great saints were martyred at this time: St George, St Panteleimon, St Dimitrios, and St Katherine in Egypt.

Repulsed by all that he saw, Constantine asked to be transferred back to the western part of the Empire, where his father was ill. Constantius proclaimed his son, Constantine, Emperor in York in 306 to help him in the power struggle against Maxentius and so that he would follow him as ruler. Constantius died in battle but Constantine decided to continue to fight Maxentius. He took two Legions, crossed the Alps and entered North Italy. Meanwhile Galerius decided he wanted to be the supreme ruler of the Empire and led his legions to Salonica, heading for Rome. He died on the journey.

The armies of Constantine and Maxentius met at the Milvian Bridge. On the eve of the battle, Constantine and his soldiers saw a cross in the rays of the sun with the words 'By this sign you will conquer'. He knew this was the Christian's symbol. The next day Constantine's soldiers went into battle with a cross as their standard and won (12 October 312).

The Empire was huge so Licinius, Constantine's brother-in-law, ruled the eastern part while Constantine had the west. The first act the two did together was to issue *The Edict of Milan*. This allowed Christians to worship freely. People could become Christians and they could build Churches.

Licinius soon broke this agreement and started to kill Christians again so Constantine took his army and defeated Licinius. Constantine then united the Roman Empire and became the sole ruler. He had a great army and navy.

He established a Christian Empire which became so strong that it lasted more than 1,000 years. He built many Churches. He had Bibles bound in gold placed in 50 Churches in his new Christian city, Constantinople.

Constantine the Great shares his feast day, 21st May, with his mother, St Helen.

## The First Oecumenical Council

The Emperor Constantine called a meeting of 318 Bishops in Nicea in the year 325 as there was an argument in the Church about *whether there was ever a time when the Son of God did not exist.* Arius could not believe that the Son of God had always been with the Father and the Holy Spirit. This means that the Son of God is one of the persons of the Holy Trinity: Father, Son and Holy Spirit: One God. The meeting proclaimed that Arius was wrong. The first part of the Creed we say in the Liturgy was written at this Council.
