## Questions

- Which Saint or Feast are you named after?
- Find out about your Saint or Feast. When is the feast day?

## Know Your Own Saint

Most Orthodox Christians are named after a saint or a special feast (Theophania, after the feast of Theophany, Christ’s Baptism). This means that they have a Name Day as well as a birthday. The Saint will keep a special watch over the people who have taken their name, especially if they are honoured in return, and will respond to prayers. Their life and achievements will be an example to follow.

It is important to have an icon of your own Saint or Feast, to know and keep the feast day, and to find out as much as possible about them.

## For example: St Luke

Feast Day: 18th October

St Luke is known as the Apostle and Evangelist, Physician and, in tradition, the first iconographer to paint an icon of the Mother of God.

This is because he is considered to be one of the 70 Apostles whom Christ sent out in twos to announce Salvation in towns and villages.

He is also considered to be the author of the Gospel according to Luke and the Acts of the Apostles.

Luke was in Jerusalem at the time of Christ’s Passion and Crucifixion and was on his way to Emmaus with Cleopas when Christ appeared to them as they walked along the road. They only realised who was talking to them when He broke bread and blessed it. Christ immediately disappeared but the Disciples hurried back to Jerusalem to tell all the others that they had seen Jesus.

St Luke came from Antioch and met St Paul there when he returned. He journeyed with St Paul, eventually to Rome. It was here that St Paul asked him to write his Gospel.

After St Paul’s death St Luke became Bishop of Thebes, where he ordained priests and deacons to help establish churches and look after the needs of the Christians there.

He was 84 when the pagans crucified him on an olive tree.
