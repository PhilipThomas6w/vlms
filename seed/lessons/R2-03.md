- What does each of these four Commandments mean to us?

### The Ten Commandments: Exodus 20:1-12

The first four Commandments were given to Moses by God on Mount Sinai.

1. I am the Lord thy God, thou shalt have no other Gods before me.
2. Thou shalt not make unto thyself any graven image.
3. Thou shalt not take the name of the Lord thy God in vain.
4. Remember the Sabbath day to keep it holy.

The next six concern your neighbour.

5. Honour thy father and mother.
6. Thou shalt not kill.
7. Thou shalt not commit adultery.
8. Thou shalt not steal.
9. Thou shalt not bear false witness.
10. Thou shalt not covet.

## The First Four Commandments are Directly to do with God

**1. I am the Lord thy God. Thou shalt have no other Gods before me.**

God is the only God. There is only one God.

**2. Thou shalt not make unto thyself any graven image.**

Don’t be tempted to treat anything else as being as important as God.

**3. Thou shalt not take the Name of the Lord thy God in vain.**

Don’t be disrespectful in any way to God.

**4. Remember the Sabbath day to keep it holy.**

Keep Sunday as a day especially for God; try to go to church.

**Make God Number 1 in your life.**

## Notes for the lesson

- The first four commandments have to do with our friendship with God and the last six with our friendship with people. This lesson looks only at the first four.
- Read them, and ask the children what they understand from them.
- Ask whether they can be written in a positive way, instead of “Thou shalt not…”. For example:
  1. Worship only God.
  2. Make God most important (or keep God on the throne).
  3. Respect God and His Name.
  4. Celebrate God in church (or keep Sunday special).
- At a later stage the children can memorise the traditional version.
- The children can write their version on a tablet shape which they cut out and glue into their books. If you want them to have the traditional version too, photocopy a tablet with it already typed on, to cut out and glue beside the other one in the scrap book.
- The other important thing to communicate is why God gave the commandments to Moses to give the Israelites.
- It can help to compare them with the Highway Code: the rules are not there to spoil our fun but to keep us safe (to protect us from harming ourselves and others) and to show us how to live life well. If we put God first, ultimately we can know all will go well and enjoy friendship with Him.
