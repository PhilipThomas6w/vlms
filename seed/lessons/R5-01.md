## The Prosphora and Proskomede

Prosphora means gift. It is the gift brought to the Church by a parishioner to be used in the Liturgy. It is made with love and prayers using flour, yeast, water and salt. Any Orthodox Christian can make the Prosphora, but it must arrive in Church before the service of the Proskomede or Preparation, which is prayed by the priest before the Liturgy, often before the congregation arrives. It usually takes about half an hour.

During this service pieces of bread are cut out of the loaf using the stamp that has been pressed into the dough to leave a clear design.

First is cut the square piece known as the Lamb. This will become the Body of Christ, then a triangular piece is cut out for the Mother of God. After this, small triangles for the saints and crumbs for each person to be remembered at the Liturgy will be taken. Everything is placed on a discos and covered with a “star” and veil.

When the Prosphora has been prepared the wine and water are poured into the chalice and covered with another veil. This will become the blood of Christ later in the service.

Finally, the priest censes the gifts to demonstrate their holiness in being part of the most important service, the Liturgy.

## The Rite of Preparation

At a side table, known as the *Table of Oblation*, the priest

- Recites prayers of personal preparation while vesting, and
- Prepares the bread and wine for the celebration of the Divine Liturgy. The priest cuts a cube of bread, referred to as the *Lamb*, from a larger loaf known as a *prosphora*. He places this bread on the *diskos* in commemoration of Jesus Christ. It is the *Lamb*, which will later become the Body of Christ and offered to the faithful in Holy Communion. Around the Lamb the priest places smaller particles of bread in prayerful remembrance of the saints, the living and the faithful departed. In current practice, the Rite of Preparation, which is sometimes called the *Proskomidia* or *Prothesis*, takes place prior to the public portion of the Divine Liturgy.

## Prosphora Baking is a Prayer of Intercession

**You need:**

- An icon
- A lamp or candle
- A censer, charcoal and incense
- Holy water
- Pen and paper for your list of people for whom you are praying

**Ingredients**

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Strong White Flour | 12 oz |
| Dried Yeast | 1 tsp |
| Salt | ½ tsp (approx) |
| Warm Water | 6 fluid oz (approx) |

**Method**

- Mix flour, yeast and salt thoroughly
- Make a well in the bowl and pour in the water
- Draw in the rest of the flour from the sides of the bowl and make a soft dough. (Add more water a little at a time if necessary).
- Lightly flour your work surface. Tip out the dough.
- Knead the dough for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
- Shape dough into round with a smooth flat top.
- Place into a well floured baking tin.
- Flour the Prosphora seal, place it in the centre of the dough and press it firmly down. Flatten the edges outwards and press down again.
- Slightly rock the seal and withdraw it carefully.
- Prick the dough down to the tin to help it rises evenly.
- Sprinkle with Holy Water saying “In the Name of the Father, and the Son and the Holy Spirit.”
- Cover with a clean tea towel and leave in a warm place to rise until about double the size
- Preheat the oven to 375° F, (190 C, gas mark 5).
- Bake for about 30 minutes. It is important that the prosphora is well cooked so that the priest is able to remove the Lamb easily. (Check for a hollow sound when tapped on the bottom, especially in the centre.)
- Place on a wire rack and cover with the clean tea towel to avoid the crust becoming too hard.

The Priest needs the prosphora to be in the altar at church at least 30 minutes before the Liturgy begins for the preparation service.
