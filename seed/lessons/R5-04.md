## Justinian I the Great

![Mosaic of the Emperor Justinian, crowned and haloed, with a jewelled brooch at his shoulder](justinian-mosaic.jpg)

Justinian was:

- Born around 482 died 14th November 565
- Succeeded his uncle Justin I (450 – 527)
- Emperor from 1 August 527

“An individual of medium height, with a round ruddy face, approachable and gentle. Simple in his tastes, indifferent to splendour and able to work day and night.”

He surrounded himself with able administrators and generals and it was his ambition to restore the Roman Empire to its original borders. Under the general Belisarios the barbarians were defeated and North Africa and Italy were re-conquered. Other generals took back parts of Spain and Sardinia.

![Map of the Byzantine Empire in AD 565: the empire of 527 around the eastern Mediterranean, from Constantinople and Anatolia to Antioch, Jerusalem and Egypt, and the lands added by 565 in Italy, North Africa around Carthage and southern Spain around Córdoba](map-of-the-empire.jpg)

![Six gold coins of Justinian: on one side the emperor, crowned and holding a cross, with his name in Latin; on the other an angel holding a cross and an orb, inscribed VICTORIA AUGG](justinians-coins.jpg)

Justinian was responsible for many major building works including the Great Church of Ayia Sophia in Constantinople as well as churches in Ravenna and Jerusalem. He also built several monasteries the most famous of which was that at Mt Sinai which still exists.

![Drawing of the Great Church of Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, its great dome rising above half-domes and arched windows, with trees in front](hagia-sophia.jpg)

![Mosaic of the Emperor Justinian carrying a golden bowl, with Archbishop Maximian holding a jewelled cross, clergy with a Gospel book and a censer, officials, and guards with a shield bearing the Chi-Rho, from the Church of San Vitale, Ravenna](justinian-and-his-court.jpg)

![The walled Monastery of St Catherine at the foot of Mount Sinai, seen from above, with its church, bell tower and cypress trees](st-catherines-sinai.jpg)

He was responsible for a full revision of Roman Law in his Institutes. These are still studied by lawyers in Europe and elsewhere to this day

He was himself, a learned theologian and called an Ecumenical Council in Constantinople in 553 which produced the final definition to counter the heretic Nestorius. The Council describes Christ, the second person of the Trinity as truly God and truly man in one hypostatic union without confusion.

He caught but recovered from an outbreak of plague in 541 – 544, though his wife **Theodora** did not.

Theodora is said to have been a low-class entertainer but she became a very powerful woman who was a great supporter of her husband.
