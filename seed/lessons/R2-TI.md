## Pentecost
This session is to familiarise the children with the feast of Pentecost. The document in the folder *Pentecost* provides helpful questions for engaging with this topic. It will also be worth looking at:

- What it means for the Holy Spirit to be another ‘comforter’, and what this says about His status.

- How Pentecost serves as a reversal of the Tower of Babel story (Genesis 11:1-9). It may be worth reading St. Gregory Nazianzus’ *On Pentecost* (especially the last few chapters), and presenting this in a more digestible way to the children. Here is a link to his homily: [CHURCH FATHERS: Oration 41 (Gregory Nazianzen) (newadvent.org)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310241.htm).

- Discuss what it means for us to be a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19) through baptism and chrismation.

## St Stephen and the Martyrs
This session is to provide the children with an introduction to martyrdom. Key questions to engage with are:

- What makes someone a martyr?

- Who was persecuting the Christians and why?

To explore these questions, reference to St Stephen and St Polycarp should be made. The letters of St Ignatius of Antioch should also be made. Principally, talk about the language which surrounds martyrdom. It is described as that which enables them to become a golden loaf, as an offering to Christ, who is the bread of life (John 6.35). Notice the other parallels between the life of Christ and the martyrdom of Polycarp. Ultimately, martyrdom is the manifestation of a life truly imitative of the life of Christ, hence they image Him, the bread of life, as a perfectly baked loaf.

- [CHURCH FATHERS: Epistle to the Romans (St. Ignatius) (newadvent.org)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0107.htm) – chapter 4 and 7.

- [CHURCH FATHERS: Martyrdom of Polycarp (newadvent.org)](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0102.htm) – especially chapter 15.

Note also the value placed on the relics of these saints, and how they are treated as fine jewels.

## The Ten Commandments
In this lesson, begin with the story of how the Ten Commandments were received. The document, *10 commandments* should be consulted and used to help lead the session. In addition, it is worth considering how the 10 Commandments are summed up in Christ’s command love God with all our minds, souls and bodies and love our neighbours as ourselves. (Exodus 20; Matthew 22:37-39).

## Theophany
In addition to following the guidance of the document for this session, also consider how Theophany parallels the creation story in Genesis 1, and how this points towards the world being recreated through Christ. This is why He is baptised; not for His own benefit, but by His descent into the waters, He sanctifies all of creation.

## Communion
In addition to following the guidance of the document for this session, you could also discuss what the Passover was, and how Christ, as the Lamb of God, now enables death to pass over us.

## Feasting and Fasting
After covering the spiritual benefits of appropriately feasting and fasting, cut out the templates provided in this lessons folder, and use them to create a dial that outlines which foods are allowed on each day of a normal liturgical week. This session should also incorporate the document on grace before meals.

## Moses in Egypt
Present the children with the story provided in the folder for the session, using the images to illustrate the narrative’s events. Then, use the quiz at the end to put their listening skills to the test!

## Know your Saint!
If possible, teachers should learn a bit about the saints of their respective pupils. Teachers can also present on their own saints, and engage with the children to share what they know of their own patrons. This lesson should be interactive, and encourage the children to want to know more about their own saint. Perhaps creating a comic strip of their saint’s life would be an activity worth considering.
